# Day 8 design notes

Concept: what if the MBTA covered all of New England.

## Decisions from Max

- Today's MBTA subway and commuter rail stay as they are. Everything else builds off them.
- Extensions come from real proposals first, then from what makes sense. Research comes before drawing.
- Small subway systems for cities like Portland, Worcester, Hartford, Springfield, Manchester/Nashua and Burlington.
- Reaching every town is not a goal.
- **The Bigger Dig:** the tunnel connecting North Station and South Station, "the hardest engineering challenge in the modern era."
- **Boston inset:** a box zoomed in on Boston showing an extended version of the current MBTA, and highlighting the Bigger Dig. This is the map's hook.

- **Secondary map:** an updated T map in the style of the big official maps that hang in stations, showing the full fantasy T: the extensions, any Yellow Line and the Bigger Dig. Fan map: no official MBTA logo, and clearly labeled as not official.

- **Boston Central:** the new main station, in the middle of the Bigger Dig tunnel, at State Street (Max's decision). It's about halfway between North and South Station, on Blue and Orange, 509 ft from Government Center (straight-line distances from MBTA stop coordinates). The site is our invention; the state's four-track design used Aquarium. Buildability hasn't been checked. Boston Central needs the tunnel, so they are one project. The map shows it as the region's main hub.
- **North and South Station stay open** but stop being major terminals. Trains run through them to Boston Central and beyond. The tunnel has four tracks.
