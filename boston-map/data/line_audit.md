## Tram network (November 7, 2026)

Every tram is **invented**: a NERTA tram on a real MBTA bus corridor. NERTA has no buses; trams run on today's busiest bus streets and pods do the rest. Data: `boston-map/data/tram/tram_draft.json` (all 32 routes; the 13 first approved plus the 19 Max approved late on November 7). The stops were planned for NERTA, not copied from today's bus stops (Max: "new stops and stuff might change bus routes so we can't exactly use the current stops as is"): a stop at every NERTA station within 275 m of the route (400 m at a route end), a shared stop where two trams cross or run together, and stops at real squares, village centers or main cross streets about every 650 m between. Stops on the same street are the same stop on every tram, with one name and one spot.

**Sources.** Corridors: the MBTA V3 API (route, shape and name, pulled November 7) and Wikipedia's *List of MBTA bus routes* (read November 8); they agree on both ends of all 32. Stops: every stop's spot is NERTA planning, logged as "invented stop on a real street". Its *name* needs two sources: OpenStreetMap for the street or square, plus a matching name on any MBTA bus stop within 300 m (V3 API, all 6,875 bus stops, pulled November 8) or a Wikipedia article. NERTA stations keep the sources in `stations_real.geojson`; where that file has only one, the tram data adds OSM, Wikipedia or an MBTA stop name when found. A station stop sits at the station's spot in `stations_real.geojson`, so it can be up to 275 m off the tram's street (400 m at a route end). Six spots that were street centroids or off the square were moved to where the tram meets the line: Melnea Cass and Northampton on the 1, Uphams Corner on the 15 and 16, Grove Hall on the 23, Seaver St on the 16 and 22. Each stop in the json carries `sources`, `verified`, `note` (which sources and how far apart), `basis`, `placement`, `today` and `shared_with`.
