## How to read this file

Every fact carries one of these labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **VERIFIED** | Exists or happened, with 2 independent sources. |
| **VERIFIED (W)** | 2 sources, but one is Wikipedia. Weaker than an agency document plus a news story. |
| **ONE SOURCE** | Only 1 source found. Not accurate under Max's rule until a second turns up. |
| **PROPOSED** | Someone real proposed or studied it. It does not exist. |
| **JUDGMENT** | My opinion. Not a fact. All verdicts and all suggested stops are judgment. |
| **I DON'T KNOW** | I could not confirm it. |

Verdicts:
- **MAKES SENSE**: real demand, usable right-of-way, realistic money.
- **FANTASY ONLY**: "makes sense only in fantasy." Plausible, but needs big money or a rebuilt right-of-way.
- **DOESN'T MAKE SENSE**: weak demand or no workable route, even on a fantasy map.

Three things limit this file:
1. **mass.gov blocks direct fetching (HTTP 403).** I read the MassDOT documents as Internet Archive copies of the same PDFs. I cite the original mass.gov URLs, and they should open normally in your browser.
2. **I hit the search limit before finishing.** Items marked "not reached" were not checked, so they are gaps, not findings. See "Gaps" at the end.
3. **Populations and geography in my reasoning are from memory** unless a source is given. They are marked "(memory)". Check them before they go on the map.
4. **Not every source was read in full.** I read the MassDOT chapters, Rail Vision tables, Northern Tier report and the Harvard paper directly. Wikipedia pages and most news stories came through a fetch summary or a search summary. Before any number goes on the finished map, open the source.

### Surprises worth your time

1. **The MBTA is rewriting its regional rail plan right now.** Rail Modernization Plan virtual public meetings are listed for **November 4 and November 17, 2026**, which is tomorrow and two weeks out. VERIFIED for the plan itself (RMP; ABC26). The meeting dates are ONE SOURCE (RMP). It could change several verdicts here.
2. **The preferred MassDOT design has no Central Station.** The 2019 pick is two tracks with a stop at State-Haymarket. Central Station exists only in the four-track design. If "Central Station" is on the map, you are drawing the option MassDOT rejected. That's fine for a fantasy map, but it should be a deliberate choice.
3. **"Hardest engineering challenge of the modern era" is not supported.** MassDOT says most of the bored tunnel sits in solid argillite bedrock, with very low settlement. See section 2.8. Suggested wording is there too.
4. **Harvard's new $8.0B leaves out the $40.7B regional rail program it depends on.** The two price tags can't be compared directly.
5. **Plymouth has been closed since 2021**, even though the track and station still exist. Don't draw it as a working station without deciding to reopen it.
6. **Fairmount electric service has slipped to 2030**, but the MBTA's own page still says 2028.

## 2. THE KEYSTONE: North-South Rail Link

**Verdict: FANTASY ONLY.** JUDGMENT. It is the single piece that turns two half-networks into one, and every serious study says it can be built. But it needs $13B to $22B in the state's numbers, and its full benefits need a further $28.9B or more of regional rail. On a fantasy map it should be the center of everything.

### 2.1 What it is

A rail tunnel joining the two downtown terminals, which are about one mile apart at the surface. North Station serves 4 lines. South Station serves 8. VERIFIED. (HKS26; M19-13)

### 2.2 Tunnel length: use 2.5 to 2.9 miles

Four different "lengths" circulate, and they measure different things. This is the most confusing fact in the whole subject.

| Number | What it measures | Source |
|---|---|---|
| About 1 mile | Surface gap between the headhouses | HKS26, citing the 2003 DEIR |
| 2.5 mi (13,100 ft) | South/Congress, portal to portal | M19-5 |
| 2.7 mi (14,300 ft) | Central Artery, portal to portal | M19-5 |
| 2.8 mi (14,600 ft) | Four-track, from the South Bay portal | M19-5 |
| 2.9 mi (15,400 ft) | Pearl/Congress, portal to portal | M19-5 |
| 2.788 / 2.958 mi | Harvard two-track / four-track corridor | HKS26 |
| 5.40 to 5.77 route mi | MassDOT full alignment including approaches | M18-DEC |

VERIFIED for about 2.7 to 2.8 miles (M19-5; HKS26; also NSRLO and BOS24). Wikipedia's "about 1.5 miles" did not match any primary document. I would not use it.

The cost gap partly comes from this. MassDOT said Harvard's 2017 number omitted a launch pit and assumed 2.7 miles of tunneling versus 5 miles in the state study. ONE SOURCE (BEA18).

### 2.4 Tracks and the preferred option

- **Preferred: two tracks, 51-ft bore, South/Congress.** VERIFIED. (M18-SEC; M19-8)
- **MassDOT rejected four tracks.** The two-track option scored higher because of lower overall cost. ONE SOURCE (M19-8).
- **The preferred option doesn't rule out four tracks later.** It leaves room for the Fairmount and Old Colony lines in a separate Central Artery tunnel. ONE SOURCE (M19-8).
- **Earlier concept:** two 41-ft deep bores, up to four tracks, 130 ft maximum depth, three portals (Back Bay, Old Colony, Fairmount). ONE SOURCE (W-NSRL).

### 2.5 Stations, including Central Station

- **New underground South Station.** Central Artery versions: under Fort Point Channel, 1,050-ft platforms. South/Congress version: inside the bore, 1,320-ft platforms, tied to the Red and Silver concourse. ONE SOURCE (M19-5). NSRLO also gives 1,050 ft.
- **New underground North Station.** Central Artery versions: mined under I-93, southeast of the surface station, with access by TD Garden and Haymarket. ONE SOURCE (M19-5).
- **State-Haymarket** replaces North in the preferred plan, connecting Green, Orange and Blue. ONE SOURCE (M19-5).
- **Central Station (four-track only).** VERIFIED. Mined under I-93 / Atlantic Avenue, south of State Street, under the Blue Line at Aquarium. Possible entrance at India Street and a possible headhouse at Long Wharf. (M19-5; MOU26 confirms "adjacent to the Aquarium Blue Line station")
- **Two-station versus three-station.** Every option has two stations except the four-track. ONE SOURCE (M18-DEC).

**The Central Station is the only option linking the Blue Line to commuter rail.** ONE SOURCE (W-NSRL), though the geometry in M19-5 supports it.

**Note for your "hardest engineering challenge of the modern era" line.** JUDGMENT. I couldn't support it, and would not print it as fact.
- MassDOT found "no significant barriers" to most alignments, and most of the bore is in good rock.
- Crossrail tunneled 25.5 miles (M18-DEC), against about 3 here. It is not the longest or deepest job of its kind.
- It is hard. MassDOT says its bores have 2 to 3 times the cross-section of other US tunnels (M18-DEC), and it threads under nearly every subway line downtown.
- "The Bigger Dig" works as a nickname. I found no source using that phrase. BOS24 gives the Big Dig about $24B.
- Safer wording: **"The hardest job left in Boston."**

### 2.9 What it would allow: through-running

- **Rail ridership.** MassDOT 2040 forecast: 150,000 no build, 195,000 with South Station Expansion or a basic two-track tunnel, 225,000 two-track with all-day peak service, 250,000 four-track. VERIFIED (BEA18; M19-67).
