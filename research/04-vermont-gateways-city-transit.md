## How to read this file

- **VERIFIED** = two independent sources agree. Both URLs are given.
- **ONE SOURCE** = I found only one. Not "accurate" under Max's two-source rule.
- **PROPOSED** = a real plan, study or bill. It has not been built.
- **JUDGMENT** = my opinion, based on the facts around it.
- **I DON'T KNOW** = I looked and could not confirm it.

Verdict words are the three you set: MAKES SENSE, MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY, DOESN'T MAKE SENSE. **All verdicts are JUDGMENT.**

Source warning: Wikipedia and its copies do not count as two sources. Where a fact rests on Wikipedia alone, it says ONE SOURCE, even when Wikipedia cites the Census or APTA. APTA's own PDFs and the mass.gov pages refused automated fetching (see Part 6). A few items were seen only in search excerpts because the page itself would not load, and those say so.

## Vermont baseline facts

- **VERIFIED:** Vermont has about 570-580 active rail miles, and about 300 are state-owned. The December 2025 Rail Plan table says 570.3 total, 297.6 state.
  - [Vermont Rail Plan, Dec 2025](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Transportation/Agency%20of%20Transportation/Program%20Updates/W~Dan%20Delabruere~Vermont%20Rail%20Plan~4-29-2026.pdf)

## V1. Vermonter, Springfield to St. Albans

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It exists, it is the backbone of Vermont service, and the track is real. Keep it, then speed it up and add trains.

- **VERIFIED:** St. Albans is the end of the line. [WCAX, Sep 2026](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/11/officials-work-restore-rail-service-between-vermont-quebec/); [VTDigger, Aug 2026](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/17/new-funds-for-alburgh-rail-bridge-could-boost-future-amtrak-link-to-montreal/)
- **ONE SOURCE:** Owners by segment. MassDOT owns Springfield to Northfield MA. NECR owns Northfield to St. Albans. FY25 riders 115,940, which excludes trips between New Haven and Washington. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermonter_(train)). Amtrak's Vermont report gives 109,136 for FY24, so the two counts differ.
- **PROPOSED:** Corridor ID aims to cut 90 minutes from Springfield to St. Albans. (Rail Plan.)
- **Stops (real):** St. Albans, Essex Junction, Waterbury, Montpelier-Berlin, Randolph, White River Junction, Windsor, Claremont NH, Bellows Falls, Brattleboro, Greenfield, Northampton, Holyoke, Springfield.

## V2. St. Albans to Montreal

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It is the state's top passenger priority, and it links a metro far bigger than anything in Vermont. The blockers are customs and paperwork more than track. It adds riders on modest track work, and a second daily train would more than double the route.

- **VERIFIED:** Service ended in 1995 and has not returned. [VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/17/new-funds-for-alburgh-rail-bridge-could-boost-future-amtrak-link-to-montreal/); [WCAX](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/11/officials-work-restore-rail-service-between-vermont-quebec/)
- **VERIFIED:** The East Alburgh Bridge over Lake Champlain is privately owned by NECR, over 100 years old and limited to 5 mph. A federal planning grant of about $4M was announced in August 2026. It pays for planning, not construction. [VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/17/new-funds-for-alburgh-rail-bridge-could-boost-future-amtrak-link-to-montreal/); [Trains In The Valley, $3.95M](https://trainsinthevalley.org/vermonter-extension-to-montreal)
- **ONE SOURCE:** Owners. NECR from St. Albans to the border; CN from the border to Montreal. CN agreed in May 2024 to restore the Rouses Point Subdivision for up to 50 mph on most of it. [Trains In The Valley](https://trainsinthevalley.org/vermonter-extension-to-montreal)
- **ONE SOURCE (official):** About 3 miles of CN track from Alburgh to the border needs continuous welded rail. (Rail Plan.)
- **ONE SOURCE, unofficial transcript:** VTrans told senators in April 2026 that the train will not reach Montreal within three years, and that Quebec's funding for the engineering step disappeared. [AI transcript](https://goldendomevt.com/meetings/Senate/Transportation/2026-04-29_09-05/transcript.html). The transcript is machine-made and garbled in places, so check the video before quoting it.
- **Blocker detail:** see section G0 (Border).
- **Stops:** St. Albans, then Montreal Central Station. The proposal calls for no stop between them, per [WCAX](https://www.wcax.com/2026/09/11/officials-work-restore-rail-service-between-vermont-quebec/).

## V3. Ethan Allen Express, Rutland to Burlington

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It exists, and Burlington's riders far exceed what Essex Junction lost to it. ([Seven Days](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/burlingtons-amtrak-train-service-is-off-to-a-strong-start-37484535), ONE SOURCE, seen in a search excerpt.)

- **VERIFIED:** Revenue service to Burlington began July 29, 2022. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Allen_Express); [WCAX 2022](https://www.wcax.com/2022/07/29/new-amtrak-train-route-roll-burlington-new-york-city/)
- **VERIFIED:** Riders about 85,000. [Wikipedia, 85,314 FY25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Allen_Express); [WCAX 2026](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/27/vermont-sees-40-jump-amtrak-ridership-since-before-pandemic/)
- **ONE SOURCE:** Rutland to Burlington upgrade cost $26M. Vermont Railway owns Burlington to Whitehall. Total trip about 7 hours for 310 miles. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Allen_Express)
- **Stops (real):** Burlington, Ferrisburgh-Vergennes, Middlebury, Rutland, Castleton, then NY.
- **PROPOSED, advocate:** Vermonters for People-Oriented Places argue for a Montreal-NYC service via Rutland and Burlington, with 5 round trips a day at 60 mph or 9 at 80 mph. [VPOP slides, 2024](https://legislature.Vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/Senate%20Transportation/Agency%20of%20Transportation/W~Michael%20Arnold~Vermont%20Rail%20Corridor~2-29-2024.pdf). The state Rail Plan records this idea but does not recommend it. ONE SOURCE, and it is a rough gravity model.

## V4. Burlington to Essex Junction (Winooski Branch)

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It is the cheapest high-value piece of track in the state. Eight miles joins Vermont's two Amtrak lines and its two busiest stations, and removes the reason each line stops short.

- **VERIFIED:** About 7.7 to 8 miles, needs rebuilding, and the 2017 study priced track and bridges at $2.5M per mile, about $19.25M. [VermontBiz 2019](https://vermontbiz.com/node/234551); [VermontBiz 2019](https://vermontbiz.com/node/232375)
- **ONE SOURCE (official):** Owner is NECR, track is Class 1. Upgrading the bridges and track to 286,000-pound freight standard is a Tier 1 initiative. Extending the Ethan Allen to Essex Junction is Tier 3. (Rail Plan.)
- **ONE SOURCE, source page not pinned down:** A search excerpt said the 2017 study proposed an "Essex South" stop because the Amtrak station is on the wrong side of the junction for Burlington trains. I could not re-find the page, so treat this as a lead. The study slides show the concept only as an image.
- **Stops:** Burlington Union Station, Winooski, Essex South, Essex Junction.

## V5. Burlington to Essex Junction to St. Albans

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It was the best performer in the state's own study, it uses a track Amtrak already runs on, and it runs the direction people actually commute. Warning: Burlington's last commuter train failed, so this needs all-day service and proper connections.

- **ONE SOURCE (official), PROPOSED:** 2017 study by HDR for VTrans, four options. [Slides](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2018/WorkGroups/House%20Transportation/Montpelier-St.%20Albans%20Rail/W~Costa%20Pappis~Montpelier-%20St.%20Albans%20Rail%20Feasibility%20Study~1-25-2017.pdf)

| Option | Capital | Operating | Annual support | Daily riders |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Corridor-wide, 6 round trips | $301M | $4.9M | $3.7M | 930 |
| Corridor-wide, 11 round trips | $363M | $8.9M | $6.5M | 1,835 |
| St. Albans line only | $164M | $2.8M | $1.4M | 1,140 |
| Montpelier line only | $249M | $6.1M | $5.1M | 695 |

- **ONE SOURCE (official):** Daily commuters St. Albans to Burlington 4,433; Burlington to St. Albans 548. Montpelier to Burlington 1,737; Burlington to Montpelier 1,096. (Same slides.)
- **VERIFIED:** Critics said the study overpriced things. Its $162-189M for trainsets assumed locomotive-hauled trains. Self-propelled diesel cars could be far cheaper, and AllEarth Rail owned 12 of them. [VermontBiz](https://vermontbiz.com/node/232375); [VermontBiz](https://vermontbiz.com/node/234551). I DON'T KNOW where those cars are today.
- **VERIFIED:** Champlain Flyer, Burlington to Charlotte, ran December 2000 to February 2003, and ended because it cost more than expected and drew too few riders. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champlain_Flyer); [Charlotte News](https://www.charlottenewsvt.org/2022/08/10/after-70-years-interstate-train-returns-to-northwest-vermont/)
- **Stops (from the study fare table and excerpts):** St. Albans, Milton, Essex Junction, Essex South, Winooski, Burlington Union Station. GlobalFoundries in Essex is a large employer next to the track, and critics said the study missed it. [VermontBiz](https://vermontbiz.com/node/232375)
- **ONE SOURCE, PROPOSED, advocate:** A 2014 advocates' paper listed a longer diesel-car line: Charlotte, Shelburne, South Burlington, Burlington Union Station, Winooski, St. Michael's/Fanny Allen, Essex Junction, IBM Tech Park, Richmond, Bolton, Waterbury, Montpelier State House. [Blog copy](https://tonyrvt.blogspot.com/search/label/commuter%20rail%20feasibility%20study)

## V6. Burlington to Montpelier

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** It has the worst numbers of the four options: fewest riders, most subsidy, for a Vermonter-served corridor. On a fantasy map, though, it completes the state's I-89 spine.

- Numbers are in the V5 table.
- **ONE SOURCE:** Montpelier-Berlin station is in Berlin, not downtown Montpelier, and NECR owns it. The downtown branch lost regular passenger service in 1938. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpelier_station_(Vermont))
- **Stops (from study coverage):** Burlington, Winooski, Essex South, Richmond, Waterbury, Montpelier Junction, Downtown Montpelier.

## V7. Montpelier to Barre

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** The track is there and the state owns it, but the towns are tiny. Run it only as the end of a Burlington line.

- **VERIFIED:** State-owned, about 13 miles, operated by Washington County RR, freight only. [VermontBiz](https://vermontbiz.com/node/234551); [Rail Plan lease list](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Transportation/Bills/H.488/Commitee%20of%20Conference%20Documents/H.488~Damien%20Leonard~Overview%20of%20Railway%20Leases~5-7-2025.pdf) (14 miles to Websterville).
- **ONE SOURCE:** No passenger rail since 1927. About $20M to rebuild. Barre, Montpelier and Berlin had about 19,000 residents in 2017. [VermontBiz](https://vermontbiz.com/node/234551)
- **ONE SOURCE (official):** Upgrading it for freight is Tier 3. (Rail Plan.)
- **Stops:** Montpelier Junction, Downtown Montpelier, Barre.

## V8. White River Junction

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It is a true junction of the Connecticut River and I-89 valleys, and the Rail Plan's Valley Flyer extension here beats a Montreal day train in forecast riders.

- **ONE SOURCE (official), PROPOSED:** Valley Flyer to White River Junction, 114,000 vs 81,000. Tier 3 in the plan. (Rail Plan.)
- **ONE SOURCE, unofficial transcript:** VTrans says crew hours, overnight layover, equipment and signals make this harder than it looks. [Transcript](https://goldendomevt.com/meetings/Senate/Transportation/2026-04-29_09-05/transcript.html)
- **ONE SOURCE, PROPOSED:** A second train to White River Junction appeared in the Corridor ID planning, $500,000 for planning. [VermontBiz, Jan 2024](https://vermontbiz.com/node/227824)
- **Stops:** Greenfield, Brattleboro, Bellows Falls, Claremont, Windsor, White River Junction.

## V9. Concord NH to White River Junction

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** This is the direct Boston to Vermont route and the most important fantasy link on the map, but the track is gone. It needs a rebuilt right-of-way, and the rail trail would be a fight.

- **VERIFIED:** The Northern Railroad corridor was part of the 2003 Boston-Montreal study and is now largely the Northern Rail Trail. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rail_Trail); [Vermont Public](https://www.vermontpublic.org/post/regional-report-connecting-boston-and-montreal-rail)
- **ONE SOURCE:** Passenger service ended in 1965. [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Railroad_(New_Hampshire)). I DON'T KNOW whether any short pieces remain in service.
- **Stops (JUDGMENT):** Concord, Franklin, Lebanon, White River Junction.

## V10. Bellows Falls to Rutland

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** It would link the two Amtrak lines across the mountains and serve ski towns, but it is a 52-mile freight line and riders would be seasonal.

- **VERIFIED:** State-owned, leased to Green Mountain RR, about 50-52 miles. ## V11. Brattleboro

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It is the busiest single Vermont-New York pair, so more trains here are an easy win.

- **VERIFIED:** Brattleboro-NYC is the top Vermont pair. [Amtrak report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Workgroups/Senate%20Transportation/Agency%20of%20Transportation/Program%20Updates/W~Dan%20Delabruere~Amtrak%20ridership%20report,%20Rt.%203%20and%204%20Updated%20through%20February~3-27-2025.pdf); [VRAC minutes, as reported by search](https://vtrans.vermont.gov/sites/aot/files/rail/documents/rail-council/VRAC%20Draft%20Minutes%2020241112.pdf). - **ONE SOURCE, PROPOSED:** Valley Flyer to Brattleboro, 93,000 vs 81,000. (Rail Plan.)
- **ONE SOURCE:** A new $10M station with Vermont's first full-length level-boarding platform has opened. [Railway News](https://railwaynews.net/amtrak-opens-10m-vermont-station-with-level-boarding.html). The source is weak and I did not confirm the opening date.

## V12. Green Mountain Corridor: Albany to Bennington to Manchester to Rutland

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** It is real, federally recognized and uses existing track, so it is close. But the state ranks it Tier 3, it needs real money, and the forecast gain is only about 14,000 riders. In a fantasy where money flows, draw it, because it fills the biggest gap on the Vermont map.

- **VERIFIED:** Accepted into FRA's Corridor ID program in December 2023 with $500,000 for planning. New York, Albany, Mechanicville, Bennington, Manchester, Rutland, Burlington. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Allen_Express); [WNYT](https://wnyt.com/?p=509163)
- **ONE SOURCE (official):** Bennington and the Northeast Kingdom are the two places outside 30 miles of a station. (Rail Plan.)
- **ONE SOURCE:** A stop in Bennington village itself is considered unlikely, so the stop may be North Bennington. [VermontBiz](https://vermontbiz.com/node/227824)
- **ONE SOURCE:** A 2014 study priced Albany to Rutland via North Bennington at about $138M and said it would save about 25 minutes. Route: Albany-Rensselaer, Mechanicville, Hoosick Junction, North Bennington, Manchester, Rutland. [Bennington Banner](https://www.benningtonbanner.com/local-news/new-passenger-rail-discussed-proposed-option-could-connect-north-bennington-to-albany/article_026795b1-ed35-51d9-a9f8-35078f18837a.html)
- **ONE SOURCE:** ADA platform rules would make the existing North Bennington station unusable. The study proposed a new station across the street and straightened tracks, on land mostly owned by Bennington College. [Bennington Banner, Mar 2014](https://www.benningtonbanner.com/local-news/community-input-sought-on-rail-study/article_2a452729-3549-5021-b135-3cb264970bb1.html)
- **ONE SOURCE:** The old Rutland line south of Bennington toward Chatham was abandoned in 1953. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Bennington_station). Do not draw this as an alternate route.
- **Status:** Step 1 was near conclusion in June 2025. Step 2 waits on FRA funding. (Rail Plan; [transcript](https://goldendomevt.com/meetings/Senate/Transportation/2026-04-29_09-05/transcript.html).)
- **Stops:** Albany-Rensselaer, Mechanicville, Hoosick Junction, North Bennington, Arlington (JUDGMENT; a resident objected there in 2014), Manchester, Rutland. A possible Pawlet station came up at a 2024 meeting. [ONE SOURCE](https://cpo.soar.com/articles/1477766/Bennington-County/Vermont/Push-for-New-Passenger-Rail-Service-Gains-Momentum-in-Vermont)

## V13. White River Junction to St. Johnsbury

## What the numbers say

**JUDGMENT from the table:**

1. **Density beats size.** Norfolk's urban area is 50% bigger than Buffalo's, yet Buffalo's line carries nearly three times the annual riders (2.45M vs 0.87M). Buffalo's line runs a dense corridor from downtown to a university. Norfolk's city density is about a third lower. Caution: Buffalo's weekday count conflicts, about 7,300 in Q2 2024 vs 11,800 in Q2 2026, so its "weekday per mile" in the table may be high.
2. **Every "subway" in a small city got a cheap tunnel.** Rochester and Newark used dead canal beds. No small city in this set paid full price for a tunnel and came out ahead. Buffalo's is the closest case and riders have fallen.
3. **Line shape matters most.** The best lines string together a downtown, a university and a hospital.
4. **Reused freight corridors are the bargain.** Ottawa's Trillium shows it. So does Vermont's self-propelled car argument.
5. **Streetcars cost as much per mile as light rail now.** Tacoma paid about $118M per mile and Charlotte about $125M per mile (my math). Kenosha's $3M per mile was a 2000-era heritage job and can't be repeated.

## Thresholds

| Level | Rule of thumb (JUDGMENT) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Heritage loop | City about 100,000; tourism purpose | Kenosha |
| Starter streetcar | City 200,000+ at 4,000+ per sq mi, or a core inside a bigger metro, with a strong spine | Tacoma |
| One light rail line | Urban area 500,000+, city 5,000+ per sq mi, with downtown, university and hospital on one line | Buffalo, ION, Tide as the warning |
| Light rail network | Urban area 1M+ | Salt Lake, Sacramento, Charlotte, Ottawa |
| True subway | Only with free right-of-way, or urban area well over 1M | Rochester and Newark |

# Part 4: Naming and map conventions

## How the MBTA does it

- **VERIFIED:** Four subway colors assigned August 26, 1965, by history and geography under Cambridge Seven. Red for Harvard, Blue for the harbor, Green for the Emerald Necklace, Orange for Orange Street (disputed; designer Peter Chermayeff said it was chosen mainly for color balance). Yellow was rejected after testing. [GBH](https://www.wgbh.org/news/local/2022-12-07/how-did-the-mbta-train-lines-get-their-colors); [Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/wickedpedia/2023/03/20/mbta-t-subway-lines-get-their-colors-history/)
- **ONE SOURCE:** Green Line letters were assigned in 1967, in order north to south, B through E. A closed in 1969. Red Line branches use A, B, C codes that riders rarely see. Mattapan is a red-colored line. Silver Line routes are SL1-SL5. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_nomenclature)
- **ONE SOURCE, search excerpts:** Commuter rail purple was adopted in October 1974 for looks, not meaning. Excerpts came from a group of MBTA-history pages including [Boston Magazine](https://www.bostonmagazine.com/?p=2131084), and I did not pin down which said it.
- **ONE SOURCE, search excerpt:** The T draws the Silver Line as a fifth subway line even though it is a bus.
- **ONE SOURCE:** "Indigo Line" was the proposed name for the Fairmount Line upgrade. [Wikipedia copy](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/Indigo_Line)
- **ONE SOURCE:** Commuter lines are named by end points or corridors with slashes, split by North and South Station: Fitchburg, Haverhill, Lowell, Newburyport/Rockport; Fairmount, Fall River/New Bedford, Framingham/Worcester, Franklin/Foxboro, Greenbush, Kingston, Needham, Providence/Stoughton. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_nomenclature)

## How regional rail maps show through-running

- **ONE SOURCE:** SEPTA's 1989 map used one color per through route across Center City, after the tunnel opened in 1984. Colors had to contrast or riders could not see where routes changed. R1-R8 numbers ended in 2010. [Transit Map blog](https://transitmap.net/septa-regional-rail-1989/); [Wikipedia copy](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/List_of_SEPTA_Regional_Rail_stations)
- **VERIFIED:** SEPTA Metro uses one letter and one color per line (L blue, B orange, T green, G yellow, D pink, M purple), with numbered sub-routes like B1, B2, B3. [Patch](https://patch.com/pennsylvania/philadelphia/septa-rolls-out-new-colorcoded-letterbased-wayfinding-system); [WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/septa-subway-lines-name-changes/)
- **ONE SOURCE, search excerpt:** Paris RER uses letters A-E, and lines run through the city across operators. [Works in Progress](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/the-magic-of-through-running/)
- **ONE SOURCE:** Munich numbers its S-Bahn lines, S1-S8, all sharing one central tunnel, which joins 12 older branches. [Works in Progress](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/the-magic-of-through-running/)
- **ONE SOURCE, PROPOSED:** North South Rail Link would remove the one-mile gap between North and South stations. [TransitMatters](https://transitmatters.org/north-south-rail-link). An August 2026 Harvard paper studies its costs. [HKS](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/missing-mile-evaluating-economic-costs-and-benefits-north-south-rail-link-and-regional)

## Recommended rules for the fantasy map (JUDGMENT)

1. **Leave today's map alone.** Red, Orange, Blue, Green, Silver and purple commuter lines stay as drawn.
2. **Extensions keep the parent name.** A Lowell Line to Manchester and Concord becomes "Lowell/Concord," following the slash style.
3. **Keep purple for regional rail.** For a 40-line map, add shades only for groups. Purple has to mean regional rail.
4. **Use the Munich and RER idea for through-running.** Give a through route one continuous color and a short code, for example R1 Worcester to Lowell. Do this only if the map assumes the Rail Link.
5. **Green Line letters are free.** A is vacant, and F onward is unused. Keep north-to-south logic within any new Green group.
6. **New city systems should look local.** Worcester's lines can use a "W" prefix (WA, WB) in a shared color, in the way the Mattapan Line sits under Red. Hartford could get H1, H2.
7. **Avoid "Green Line" for Burlington.** Use "Queen City Line" (JUDGMENT) or similar.
8. **Unused real names.** Indigo is the one with a real MBTA history. Yellow carries the story of being rejected in 1965.
9. **BRT stays Silver.** Show CTfastrak as Silver, which matches the MBTA's real habit of showing BRT as a line.
10. **Off-map arrows.** Use arrows with city names for Albany, New York, Montreal and Quebec City, and a small customs mark on any Canada-bound line.
