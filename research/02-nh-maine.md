**How to read the labels**
- **VERIFIED**: 2 independent sources agree.
- **ONE SOURCE**: only 1 source found. Treat as unverified.
- **CONFLICT**: sources disagree. Both values are given.
- **PROPOSED**: someone has proposed it. It is not built or funded.
- **MY JUDGMENT**: my opinion, not a sourced fact. All verdicts and suggested stops are in this category.
- **I don't know**: I could not confirm it.

**Read "Research limits" at the end first.** Most official PDFs were blocked, so many study numbers come from news reports or search excerpts, not the studies themselves.

## 1. NH Capitol Corridor (Lowell, Nashua, Manchester, Concord)

**Verdict (MY JUDGMENT):**
- **Lowell to Nashua to Manchester: MAKES SENSE.** It uses existing track to reach Manchester and Nashua, the two largest cities in this table. The Nashua segment alone was projected at 60% to 65% of the full ridership for under half the cost.
- **Manchester to Concord: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** The track exists, but Concord was the study's weakest case and the MBTA's rights historically stopped short of it.

**Verified**
- The Republican-controlled Executive Council voted 3-2 on Dec. 21, 2022, against amending the nearly $5.5M AECOM contract. That effectively ended the study and preliminary design. Sources: https://granitememo.com/executive-council-nixes-regional-commuter-rail/ and https://www.nashuatelegraph.com/news/local-news/2023/01/28/commentary-executive-council-derails-commuter-rail/ (the Telegraph piece is an opinion column and gives no count; the 3-2 count also appears in https://kuster.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=5082)
- Gov. Ayotte opposes it, calling it financially unsustainable. Sources: https://www.businessnhmagazine.com/article/transportation-special-report-the-engine-that-couldnrsquot and https://www.nashuatelegraph.com/news/2024/12/19/commuter-rail-a-hot-topic-in-citys-capital-improvement-package/
- Nashua is now pursuing a Nashua-only first segment on its own. Rep. Goodlander secured $250,000 in federal money through FY2026 appropriations. As of Aug. 4, 2026, it had not reached the city. Sources: https://nashua.inklink.news/goodlander-urges-focused-approach-as-nashua-revives-commuter-rail-effort/ and https://www.businessnhmagazine.com/article/transportation-special-report-the-engine-that-couldnrsquot (Business NH confirms the local effort, not the $250,000)
- Stations in the 2023 plan: South Nashua by Pheasant Lane Mall, Nashua Crown Street, Bedford/airport, and Manchester south of Granite Street. Sources: https://www.constructionequipmentguide.com/new-study-shows-boston-to-manchester-nh-rail-service-would-cost-782m-to-build/60174 and https://nashua.inklink.news/goodlander-urges-focused-approach-as-nashua-revives-commuter-rail-effort/ (the second covers the two Nashua stops)

## 2. Plaistow commuter rail

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE (MY JUDGMENT).** It is a short hop past Haverhill on track that already carries the Downeaster. The problem was the train storage yard, not the station.

**Verified**
- The extension stalled in 2015 after local opposition to a layover yard, over noise and pollution. Sources: https://www.businessnhmagazine.com/article/transportation-special-report-the-engine-that-couldnrsquot and https://www.citizenscount.org/news/plaistow-voters-overwhelmingly-oppose-commuter-rail-station
- Plaistow selectmen asked for a "no build" recommendation. Sources: https://whav.net/2015/03/31/plaistow-ready-to-kill-commuter-rail-extension/ and https://www.citizenscount.org/news/plaistow-voters-overwhelmingly-oppose-commuter-rail-station

**One source:** The study group favored a Westville site off Joanne Drive. One alternative put the layover on the Haverhill side of the state line (search excerpts of WHAV coverage).

**Status:** I found nothing newer than retrospective mentions, so no sign of a revival. Plaistow's population is 7,830 (ONE SOURCE).

**Suggested stop (MY JUDGMENT):** Plaistow only, with the yard kept in Massachusetts.

## 3. Amtrak Downeaster (existing)

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE (it exists).** For the fantasy map, treat it as the spine of the northern network.

**Verified**
- Five daily round trips, Boston to Brunswick. Sources: https://www.nnepra.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Downeaster-Corridor-Narrative-Application.pdf and https://www.nnepra.com/nnepra-celebrates-silver-anniversary-of-amtrak-downeaster-in-2026-marking-25-years-of-service/
- FRA awarded $48M toward moving Portland's station to the main line near Mercy Hospital and St. John Street. Sources: https://www.pressherald.com/2026/08/14/new-downeaster-station-coming-to-portland/ and https://mainebiz.biz/article/48m-in-fed-funding-advances-plan-for-new-portland-train-station/
- The move would cut the 15-minute backtrack. Portland to Brunswick would drop from 50 to 33 minutes. Sources: Press Herald above and https://www.themainewire.com/2026/08/new-downeaster-train-station-project-secures-48m-in-federal-funding/
- Ridership FY2024 record: 598,426 (NNEPRA). Sources: https://www.nnepra.com/?p=2885 and https://www.centralmaine.com/2024/08/06/downeaster-reports-record-setting-ridership-revenue-for-2024-fiscal-year

**CONFLICT, explained:** FY2025 ridership is 549,120 (Amtrak, down 7.2%) or 552,649 (NNEPRA). The fiscal years differ: Amtrak runs October to September, NNEPRA July to June.
Sources: https://www.wabi.tv/2025/11/18/ridership-down-downeaster-despite-big-year-amtrak/ and https://www.nnepra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2025-1117-Board-Packet-FINAL.pdf

**One source**
- 143 track miles owned by MaineDOT, CSX and the MBTA; up to 79 mph; about 80% of riders go to or from Boston (NNEPRA application).
- Trip time Brunswick to Boston: 3 hours 20 minutes (NNEPRA application).
- FY2025 operating cost $26.5M; over 174,000 2025 trips started or ended in NH (Business NH).
- West Falmouth station proposed at $10M to $15M (NNEPRA application).
- A sixth round trip, Brunswick to Wells, is planned. An op-ed says CSX won't allow it without the station move (Sun Journal, Sept. 2026, excerpt only; page returned 403).

**Political obstacle:** Portland officials preferred the Union Station site. The mayor now expects the rail authority's site to win. ONE SOURCE (Press Herald).

## 4. Brunswick to Rockland

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE (MY JUDGMENT).** The state owns the track, the corridor has a real anchor in Bath Iron Works, and Route 1 jams in summer. Its problem is operator politics.

**Verified**
- Midcoast Railservice ended its lease in 2024, halting the extension. Sources: https://www.mainepublic.org/business-and-economy/2024-06-13/rail-agency-stops-operations-halts-downeaster-train-extension-to-rockland and https://www.mainepublic.org/business-and-economy/2025-04-30/new-company-restarts-operations-on-rockland-rail-line
- Seasonal Maine Eastern passenger trains ran from 2003 to 2015. Sources: search excerpts from Maine legislative and news coverage. Treat as ONE SOURCE until the original is read.

**One source**
- The corridor runs about 56 miles, with stations proposed at Bath, Wiscasset, Newcastle and Rockland. Upgrades estimated at $100M to $200M, trip time two hours (NNEPRA application).
- The new operator said it would not host Amtrak's extension (https://railfan.com/maine-selects-new-operator-for-rockland-branch/).
- NNEPRA's FY26 work plan says only "Monitor Rockland Branch potential" (board packet).

**CONFLICT:** The operator is named Maine Switching Services in one report and Cumberland & Knox in another. I don't know which is current.

**Suggested stops (MY JUDGMENT):** Bath (Bath Iron Works), Wiscasset, Newcastle, Waldoboro, Rockland. Waldoboro is my addition.

**Verified**
- MaineDOT chose improved bus service over rail. Sources: https://www.maine.gov/dot/programs-services/freight/lewiston-auburn-rail-plan (excerpt only; the fetch of the rail plan page returned 403) and https://www.bangordailynews.com/2023/04/27/politics/passenger-rail-wont-expand-joam40zk0w/

- A 2019 study estimated $200M to $300M for commuter rail between the cities and Portland (search excerpt).
- The Portland-Auburn section of the St. Lawrence & Atlantic has been out of use since 2015 and was officially abandoned about a decade later (Wikipedia). **Surprising and important. Confirm before drawing.**

**Ridership:** I don't know. The study was blocked.

**Suggested stops (MY JUDGMENT):** Yarmouth, Cumberland or Gray, Auburn, Lewiston.

## 6. Augusta, Waterville and Bangor

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY (MY JUDGMENT).** These are real cities with colleges, but the state's study found rail slower than driving and very expensive. It suits an occasional regional train, not frequent service.

**Verified**
- MaineDOT's 2023 study called further study of rail to Bangor "imprudent" and recommended buses. Sources: https://www.bangordailynews.com/2023/04/27/politics/passenger-rail-wont-expand-joam40zk0w/ and https://www.bangordailynews.com/2025/03/03/politics/state-politics/how-a-new-push-for-rail-to-the-bangor-area-won-over-republican-skeptics-joam40zk0w/
- The study projected 56,000 to 80,000 annual trips in 2023. The Rail Passengers Association study projected 260,000 new first-year riders. **This is a dispute, not a settled number.** Sources: BDN 2023 above and https://wgan.com/news/074470-study-backs-extension-of-amtrak-downeaster-to-bangor/
- LD 487 (2025) was rejected 9-1 in committee. Sources: https://www.pressherald.com/2025/04/24/proposal-for-passenger-rail-to-bangor-loses-steam-in-committee/ and https://www.masstransitmag.com/rail/news/55286006/me-proposal-for-passenger-rail-to-bangor-loses-steam-in-committee (full action on the floor: I don't know)
- Cost up to $902M. Sources: BDN 2025 above and an excerpt of a MaineDOT slide deck.

**One source**
- Combined Bangor and Lewiston rail cost: $892M to $1.2B (BDN 2023).
- 2019 bus trips in the study area: 149,000, nearly twice the rail forecast (BDN 2023).
- The RPA route: Lewiston, Winthrop, Waterville, Pittsfield, Bangor, Orono (WGAN).

**Lower Road (Brunswick to Augusta)**
- State-owned, 33.5 miles. An advisory council voted 11 of 14 for interim trail conversion in 2023, and MaineDOT backed LD 29 to carry that out. Sources: excerpts of the council's majority report and MaineDOT testimony.
- Whether LD 29 passed: **I don't know.** Search budget ran out.

**Blockers:** CSX owns the main line toward Bangor (older reports name Pan Am, which CSX bought). Gov. Mills and MaineDOT favor buses. Who holds those positions after 2026: I don't know.

**Suggested stops (MY JUDGMENT):** Lewiston, Winthrop, Augusta (if the Lower Road survives), Waterville, Pittsfield, Bangor, Orono.

## 7. Portland to Montreal

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY (MY JUDGMENT).** The historic Grand Trunk route exists, but it passes through very few people. Draw it as a long-distance line, not as commuter service.

## 8. Mountain Division

**Verdict (MY JUDGMENT):**
- **Portland to Westbrook, Windham, Standish: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** Suburban growth is real, but track is largely unused west of Westbrook and a trail is planned on it.
- **Standish to Fryeburg: DOESN'T MAKE SENSE.** Thin population, and the trail is the better use.

**Verified**
- LD 404 (2023) authorized a trail from Fryeburg to Standish. Sources: https://www.bikemaine.org/2023/07/governor-signs-bill-authorizing-multi-use-trail-from-standish-to-fryeburg/ and https://www.pressherald.com/2026/07/22/5-things-to-know-about-the-mountain-divsion-trail/
- Unused in Maine since about 1983. Sources: Press Herald above and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Division
- The NH section is used by Conway Scenic Railroad, through Crawford Notch to Fabyan. Sources: same two.

**One source**
- MaineDOT bought about 40 miles, Windham to Fryeburg, in 1997. Last passenger train 1958 (Press Herald).
- Portland to Westbrook was kept by Pan Am, now CSX (Wikipedia).
- MaineDOT asked federal regulators in July 2023 to let it remove the tracks (Wikipedia).
- Trail supporters expect construction "by early 2027" (Press Herald).

**CONFLICT:** The Maine line is described as 61 miles or "more than 50 miles." The second may mean the trail, not the historic line.

## 10. Concord to Lebanon and White River Junction (Northern Line)

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY (MY JUDGMENT).** It is the only real link from Boston to the Upper Valley, and the public owns the land. But the rails are gone, and it would take a beloved trail.

**Verified**
- Built in 1847; the state acquired the line in 1996. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rail_Trail and https://www.vermontpublic.org/vpr-news/2019-10-28/on-the-trail-of-new-hampshires-northern-railroad
- The trail runs about 59 miles, Lebanon to Boscawen. Sources: same Wikipedia page and https://www.trailspotting.com/2023/05/northern-rail-trail-nh.html
- The route was part of the Boston-to-Montreal federal corridor studied in 2003, then folded into the 2016 NNEIRI study. Sources: https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/57470 and https://www.metro-magazine.com/news/fra-approves-blueprint-to-connect-boston-new-haven-to-montreal-via-rail

**Blockers:** the rail trail itself, the full track rebuild, and very low density (Lebanon 354 per sq mi). A 2003 study listed the state-owned corridor as a reason to continue, but that predates the trail's growth.

**Suggested stops (MY JUDGMENT):** Concord, Boscawen, Franklin, Andover, Canaan, Lebanon, West Lebanon, White River Junction.

## 12. Eastern Route / Hampton corridor

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY (MY JUDGMENT).** It reaches Hampton and Portsmouth directly from the Newburyport Line, and the land is public. It needs a new Merrimack River bridge and conflicts with a brand-new trail.

**Verified**

**One source**

**Proposal for the map (MY JUDGMENT): one light rail line, no subway.** Portland's density is real but small, about 3,200 per sq mi in the city and 1,660 in the urban area.
- **Line 1 (BRT corridor):** USM Gorham, Gorham Village, Westbrook, Rock Row, USM Portland, Maine Medical Center, downtown, Eastern Waterfront.
- **Optional branch:** across the bridge to South Portland, which has the second-highest density in the region.
- **Transfer:** the new main-line station by Mercy Hospital.
