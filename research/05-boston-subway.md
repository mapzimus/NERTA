# Day 8 research 05: Boston rapid transit, line by line

Scope: subway, light rail and Silver Line only. Commuter rail and the Bigger Dig belong to the other agent.
Researched 2026-11-03.

## How to read this file

- **V** = verified, 2 sources found.
- **ONE SOURCE** = only 1 source found. Do not call it accurate under the 2-source rule.
- **PROPOSED** = someone has seriously suggested it. It is not built or funded.
- **JUDGMENT** = my own reasoning, not a sourced fact. All station lists are JUDGMENT unless marked otherwise.
- **I don't know** = I looked and could not confirm it.

Verdicts: **MAKES SENSE**, **FANTASY** (makes sense only in fantasy: big money or a rebuilt right-of-way, but plausible), **DOESN'T MAKE SENSE**.

Three caveats on the sourcing:

1. Wikipedia is often one of the two sources. It is not fully independent, because it restates older reports I did not read myself. Treat a "Wikipedia + news" pair as weaker than two agency documents.
2. Pages were read through summarizing tools, so check exact wording before quoting. The Urban Ring fact sheet, the Harvard paper, and the Focus40 table were read as extracted PDF text.
3. Study numbers are forecasts from their own year, in that year's dollars. None are current costs.

## 1. Blue Line

### Lynn: MAKES SENSE

**Verified**

- Wonderland became the terminus in 1954 instead of Lynn. V: [Wikipedia Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA)), [Kaufman plan-vs-actual](https://jefftkaufman.substack.com/p/mbta-extensions-planned-and-actual)
- Recommended in the 1926, 1945-47, 1966, 1978 and 1983 plans. ONE SOURCE for the full list: [Wikipedia Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA))
- 2013 MBTA estimate: $737M to $1B. V: [Item 2019](https://www.itemlive.com/2019/07/22/43-1-billion-state-budget-includes-provision-to-study-extending-blue-line-to-lynn), [Banker & Tradesman](https://bankerandtradesman.com/?p=85653)
- The FY2020 budget required a MassDOT feasibility study due March 31, 2020. V: [State budget Section 83](https://budget.digital.mass.gov/summary/fy20/outside-section/section-83-lynn-blue-line-expansion-study), [Item 2019](https://www.itemlive.com/2019/07/22/43-1-billion-state-budget-includes-provision-to-study-extending-blue-line-to-lynn)
- Lynn: 101,253 people (2020), about 9,429 per square mile. V for population: [citypopulation](https://www.citypopulation.de/en/usa/massachusetts/), [Wikipedia Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn,_Massachusetts). ONE SOURCE for density (census.gov refused the fetch).
- Lynn's commuter rail stop had under 400 daily boardings in 2018, while many Lynn riders drove or rode to Wonderland. ONE SOURCE: [Streetsblog 2020](https://mass.streetsblog.org/2020/06/16/lynn-to-get-subway-fares-from-its-commuter-rail-stop-this-summer/)
- Blue Line Extension to Lynn is listed as a "We're Imagining (Big Ideas)" item in Focus40, so it is not a next priority. V: [MBTA Focus40 table](https://cdn.mbta.com/sites/default/files/fmcb-meeting-docs/2018/june/2018-06-18-fmcb-focus40-investment-programs-revised.pdf), [CTPS Focus40 deck](https://www.ctps.org/data/calendar/pdfs/2018/MPO_0719_Focus40_Presentation.pdf) (second link seen in search results only)

**Two routes** (ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA)))

- Beside the Newburyport/Rockport tracks through the marsh. Sen. Crighton's preferred version crosses Route 1A and runs four-plus miles to downtown Lynn: [Banker & Tradesman](https://bankerandtradesman.com/?p=85653)
- Along Revere Beach Boulevard and the Lynnway on the old Boston, Revere Beach & Lynn right-of-way.

**I don't know**

- What the 2020 study concluded, or whether it was delivered. I found no results.
- What the Lynn Transit Action Plan (final report July 2020) said about the Blue Line. [MBTA's project page](https://www.mbta.com/projects/lynn-transit-action-plan) lists bus lanes and bike lanes as what got built.
- Any recent ridership forecast. I found none.
- A "$20 billion" scenario appears in a search summary of a 2019 Globe article. ONE SOURCE, not read directly, and far out of line with every other figure. Do not use it.

**JUDGMENT.** Lynn is the largest city in the region with no rapid transit stop. The weak boarding count reflects hourly service and high fares more than low demand. Cheap frequent commuter rail could fix much of that, so this is a real argument, but the map should still show it.

**Stations, marsh route:** Wonderland, River Works (GE), West Lynn, Lynn Central.
**Stations, Lynnway route:** Wonderland, Point of Pines, Lynnway, Lynn Central.
For the map I would use the marsh route, because it is one corridor already and avoids taking the beach road.

### Salem: FANTASY

- PROPOSED before: the 1969 plan went as far as Salem. The 1974 plan listed Lynn, Salem, or Route 128 in Peabody as termini. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA))
- Salem: 44,483 people. Salem drew about 2,300 commuter rail boardings in 2018. V: [Streetsblog 2020](https://mass.streetsblog.org/2020/06/16/lynn-to-get-subway-fares-from-its-commuter-rail-stop-this-summer/) ("over 2,000"), [advocacy report on CTPS data](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/533b9a24e4b01d79d0ae4376/t/60491bd418a4d80a43c881e0/1615403996362/Newburyport+Rockport+Final.pdf)
- JUDGMENT: a real historic idea with real demand, but a subway ride from Salem to downtown would be slower than a train with few stops. **Stations:** Lynn Central, Swampscott, Salem. An optional Peabody spur matches the 1974 plan.

### Beverly: DOESN'T MAKE SENSE

- I found no study that sends the Blue Line to Beverly. Wikipedia's Blue Line page does not mention it.
- Beverly had about 1,600 commuter boardings in 2018. ONE SOURCE: [advocacy report](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/533b9a24e4b01d79d0ae4376/t/60491bd418a4d80a43c881e0/1615403996362/Newburyport+Rockport+Final.pdf)
- JUDGMENT: Beverly is where the Newburyport and Rockport branches split, so a Blue terminus there forces every Cape Ann and Newburyport rider to change trains, or forces two services onto one corridor.

### Could Blue take over the Newburyport/Rockport inner corridor? DOESN'T MAKE SENSE as a takeover

- The line was the third busiest commuter line and the busiest per mile on 2018 counts (advocacy report, ONE SOURCE: [link](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/533b9a24e4b01d79d0ae4376/t/60491bd418a4d80a43c881e0/1615403996362/Newburyport+Rockport+Final.pdf)).
- The MBTA board's 2019 vote put this line in the first phase of frequent regional rail. ONE SOURCE: [Streetsblog 2020](https://mass.streetsblog.org/2020/06/16/lynn-to-get-subway-fares-from-its-commuter-rail-stop-this-summer/)
- The Cambridge Grand Junction study rejected an extended route to Lynn because it would interfere with planned commuter rail. That suggests the corridor is not spare. ONE SOURCE: [CRA study page](https://cambridgeredevelopment.org/grand-junction-transit-feasibility-study)
- JUDGMENT: **beside it** to Lynn, yes. **Instead of it**, no. Coordinate with the commuter rail agent: Blue should stop at Lynn so the two networks meet there.

### Charles/MGH (Red-Blue Connector): MAKES SENSE

- About 0.4 miles west from Bowdoin. V: [Wikipedia Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA)), [TransitMatters](https://transitmatters.info/redblueconnector)
- The state agreed in 1991 to build it by 2011, in the Big Dig emissions settlement with the Conservation Law Foundation. EPA removed the design requirement in 2015. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA))
- Cost $850M (2021 MBTA estimate). V: [CommonWealth Beacon](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/transportation/t-estimates-red-to-blue-connector-cost-at-850m), [Wikipedia Red Blue Connector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Blue_Connector)
- Design funded to 30%, with no construction money as of 2024. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Blue_Connector). Status in 2026: I don't know.
- Bowdoin station would close. V: [Suffolk Journal](https://thesuffolkjournal.com/41190/uncategorized/mbta-proposes-red-blue-connector-project-permanent-shutdown-of-bowdoin-station), [Wikipedia Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA))
- Forecast 18,940 daily trips by 2030 (2010 draft report). ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA))
- Odd detail: in Focus40 the pedestrian link is the next priority, while the full rail connector is only a Big Idea. ONE SOURCE (primary): [MBTA Focus40 table](https://cdn.mbta.com/sites/default/files/fmcb-meeting-docs/2018/june/2018-06-18-fmcb-focus40-investment-programs-revised.pdf)

**JUDGMENT:** this is the shortest high-value tunnel on this list. A [2023 op-ed](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/opinion/the-red-blue-connector-a-half-mile-of-subway) frames it as the missing link between MBTA subway lines.

### West of Charles: FANTASY

- PROPOSED: Focus40 calls it "Blue Line Connection to Red Line and Beyond" without saying where. The 1926 and 1974 plans went to Park Street. ONE SOURCE each: [MBTA Focus40 table](https://cdn.mbta.com/sites/default/files/fmcb-meeting-docs/2018/june/2018-06-18-fmcb-focus40-investment-programs-revised.pdf), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_(MBTA)). A 2019 Globe editorial headline floated Kendall, not read in full: [Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/opinion/editorials/2019/02/07/next-stop-wonderland-maybe-kendall/JNCjpGzZ0CScUBVRO3lnVL/story.html)
- JUDGMENT: **Charles/MGH, Kendall** gives a one-seat ride from Logan to Kendall. That means a new tunnel under the Charles River. Bold and defensible, but Yellow does more for the same corridor.

## 2. Orange Line

### Roslindale: MAKES SENSE. West Roxbury / VFW Parkway: MAKES SENSE (the better version)

**Verified**

- State set aside $100,000 for a MassDOT study due June 30, 2026, the first state money for this idea. V: [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2025/12/16/orange-line-extension-roslindale-massdot-study-state-house), [Streetsblog](https://mass.streetsblog.org/2025/12/19/should-the-orange-line-to-roslindale-be-the-ts-next-rapid-transit-expansion)
- 2017 city estimate about $500M and 15+ years. TransitMatters high-end estimate $285M (2020 dollars) to West Roxbury, plus $96M to VFW Parkway. ONE SOURCE: [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2025/12/16/orange-line-extension-roslindale-massdot-study-state-house) ([GBH](https://www.wgbh.org/news/politics/2025-12-17/orange-line-extension-to-roslindale-attracting-attention) carries similar coverage, not independently checked). I did not read the original reports.
- TransitMatters argues the short Roslindale version is worse value: it needs added tracks, while converting the whole line reuses existing space, at 6-minute headways versus hourly trains. ONE SOURCE: [TransitMatters 2026](https://transitmatters.org/blog/2026/3/1/from-bridge-fires-to-fire-service-what-is-next-for-the-orange-line)
- West Roxbury transit mode share just over 14% versus 38% in Jamaica Plain. ONE SOURCE: [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2025/12/16/orange-line-extension-roslindale-massdot-study-state-house)
- Roslindale rezoned for up to 10 stories in places and nearly 600 homes. ONE SOURCE: [Streetsblog](https://mass.streetsblog.org/2025/12/19/should-the-orange-line-to-roslindale-be-the-ts-next-rapid-transit-expansion)

**I don't know:** whether MassDOT's report is out. Latest coverage I found (May 2026) said it was being finalized. One MassDOT-linked description said fall 2026. No results found.

**Stations:** Forest Hills, Roslindale Village, Bellevue, Highland, West Roxbury, VFW Parkway. These reuse Needham Line stops, which is why it is cheap.

### Needham by Orange: FANTASY. Dedham / Route 128: FANTASY

- The 1945 plan ran Forest Hills to Dedham. V: [TransitMatters 2026](https://transitmatters.org/blog/2026/3/1/from-bridge-fires-to-fire-service-what-is-next-for-the-orange-line), [Kaufman](https://jefftkaufman.substack.com/p/mbta-extensions-planned-and-actual)
- The 1966 program recommended a split Orange Line with a branch to West Roxbury or Hersey. ONE SOURCE (via a search summary of [Mount Hope station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hope_station) or [Needham Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needham_Line) on Wikipedia)
- "Forest Hills to Route 128" was still "under consideration" in 2009. ONE SOURCE: [Kaufman](https://jefftkaufman.substack.com/p/mbta-extensions-planned-and-actual)
- JUDGMENT: end Orange at VFW Parkway and give Needham to the Green Line (section 4).

### Reading: FANTASY

- Reading was in the 1945 plan and the original Haymarket North target. V: [Wikipedia Orange Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_(MBTA)), [Kaufman](https://jefftkaufman.substack.com/p/mbta-extensions-planned-and-actual)
- The MBTA bought the Reading Line in 1973 with federal help. ONE SOURCE (search summary of Wikipedia)
- A third express track was built for it. It stops at Wellington. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Orange Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Line_(MBTA))
- **Conflict between sources on why it stopped:** Wikipedia's Orange Line page says Melrose residents opposed it and preferred commuter rail. Wikipedia's [Haymarket North page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haymarket_North_Extension) says funding ran out in 1972 and mentions "a narrow pass" toward Melrose. A secondhand post cites a Dec 22, 1974 Globe article saying it was shelved. Likely both reasons apply. Do not state one as the sole cause.
- Dropped from consideration by 2009. ONE SOURCE: [Kaufman](https://jefftkaufman.substack.com/p/mbta-extensions-planned-and-actual)
- JUDGMENT: Reading has the right-of-way, but suburban density is thin. **Stations:** Oak Grove, Wyoming Hill, Melrose Highlands, Greenwood, Wakefield, Reading.

### Blue Hill Avenue to Mattapan via Nubian: FANTASY

- PROPOSED June 2026 by Councilors Worrell and Culpepper: Orange branch at Ruggles, underground stops at Nubian, MLK/Warren, Grove Hall, Franklin Park Zoo, Harvard Street, Morton Street, Walk Hill, Mattapan Square. V: [Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2026/07/24/transit-advocates-call-the-orange-line-extension-proposal-not-very-serious-two-boston-councilors-say-its-overdue/), [NBC Boston](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/politics/mattapan-blue-hill-ave-subway-orange-line/3970144/) (second seen as search result)
- No cost estimate. The mayor's office said "well over a billion." TransitMatters called it not serious and a distraction from the bus lanes. Boston.com put the timeline at 15 to 20 years. ONE SOURCE: [Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2026/07/24/transit-advocates-call-the-orange-line-extension-proposal-not-very-serious-two-boston-councilors-say-its-overdue/)
- JUDGMENT: too costly and it splits Orange frequency, but it answers the real grievance of losing the Elevated. The most honest fantasy line on this page. Draw it. Station list above is theirs.

### Everett spur: FANTASY

- Focus40 Big Idea "Orange Line Spur to Everett." ONE SOURCE (primary): [MBTA Focus40 table](https://cdn.mbta.com/sites/default/files/fmcb-meeting-docs/2018/june/2018-06-18-fmcb-focus40-investment-programs-revised.pdf)
- The Lower Mystic working group floated a spur across the Mystic, tunneling past Route 16 toward Copeland Circle in Revere. ONE SOURCE: [Everett Independent](https://everettindependent.com/?p=11333)
- JUDGMENT: I'd rather serve Everett with Yellow. If you want Orange anyway: **Sullivan, Encore/Lower Broadway, Everett Square, Glendale, Copeland Circle.**

## 3. Red Line

### Arlington Heights: MAKES SENSE. Lexington / Route 128: FANTASY

**Verified history**

- Plans before 1966 go back decades: 1926, 1945, 1947. The late 1960s plan ran along the Lexington Branch to Route 128. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_(MBTA))
- 1976 law: no transit facility within 75 yards of Arlington Catholic, won by St. Agnes Parish fighting an MBTA garage next door. Repealed in 2024. V: [Globe via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/the-boston-globe/2026/10/13/a-changed-arlington-reconsiders-the-red-line/), [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/18/arlington-red-line-extension-proposal-law-mbta)
- March 1977 nonbinding town vote rejected the project. V: [Globe via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/the-boston-globe/2026/10/13/a-changed-arlington-reconsiders-the-red-line/), [Wikipedia Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_(MBTA))
- Tally: all four options lost. Even the best-liked (Lexington) lost 8,206 to 5,143, and Alewife-only lost more than 4 to 1. ONE SOURCE: [Globe via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/the-boston-globe/2026/10/13/a-changed-arlington-reconsiders-the-red-line/)
- Why: many leaders backed stops only if the line reached Lexington, fearing traffic at a mid-town terminus. Monsignor Linnehan said it could be a lifetime before the T went beyond Arlington. The Globe also reports the town was more than 99% white in 1970, with a historian pointing to fear of social change. ONE SOURCE: same Globe piece.
- May 1977 state bill barring the extension was vetoed by Gov. Dukakis. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_(MBTA))
- 1980 law bars further Arlington extension planning unless authorized by law. V: [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/18/arlington-red-line-extension-proposal-law-mbta), [Globe via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/the-boston-globe/2026/10/13/a-changed-arlington-reconsiders-the-red-line/)

**Verified, live right now**

- Town Meeting voted 145-54 in May 2026 for a home rule petition to repeal the 1980 law. Bill HD 6313 filed September 2026. Select Board voted 4-0 for no action, arguing the law already expired. ONE SOURCE: [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/18/arlington-red-line-extension-proposal-law-mbta)
- $50,000 feasibility earmark in the FY2027 budget. V: [WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/18/arlington-red-line-extension-proposal-law-mbta), [Globe via Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/the-boston-globe/2026/10/13/a-changed-arlington-reconsiders-the-red-line/)
- No money in the MBTA's 2027-2031 capital plan. Not in its long-range plan. V: same two links.
- Advocates propose cut-and-cover on the Minuteman bike path right-of-way. ONE SOURCE: Globe piece. No cost figure exists ("substantial").
- Arlington: 46,308 people (2020), about 9,000 per square mile. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Massachusetts) (which gives two density figures). WBUR rounds to about 45,000.

**JUDGMENT.** Arlington is as dense as Lynn and Alewife is at its border, so it passes. The new fight will be the bike path, since it is now beloved and sits on the corridor. Density should thin out toward Lexington and Route 128, though I did not pull a Lexington figure. **Stations:** Alewife, East Arlington (Lake Street), Arlington Center, Brattle, Arlington Heights. Fantasy add-ons: Lexington Center, Route 128.

### Mattapan heavy rail conversion: FANTASY

- A rapid transit extension to Mattapan was approved March 23, 1923, then built as a streetcar line in 1929 because of cost. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Mattapan Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattapan_Line)
- MBTA ruled out heavy rail in 2019 over cost and logistics, chose light rail, and plans Type 9 cars. V: [WBUR 2019](https://www.wbur.org/news/2019/01/28/mbta-ashmont-mattapan-trolley-plan), [CommonWealth Beacon](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/transportation/t-analysis-time-for-long-goodbye-to-mattapan-trolleys/) (read via search summary), Wikipedia for Type 9.
- Length about 2.5 miles, about 2,807 daily riders (July 2026). ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattapan_Line)
- A commentary claims a one-seat Red Line would save six minutes. ONE SOURCE, opinion: [Dorchester Reporter](https://dotnews.com/node/139426)
- Fun detail: Milton's filing to the state argues the trolley should not count as rapid transit. ONE SOURCE: [Milton filing](https://miltonma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/6366/20230915_PBReclassificationRequest_MBTACommunities)
- JUDGMENT: ridership does not justify it alone. It only pays off with a through ride. **Stations stay the same**, Ashmont through Mattapan.

### Hingham / Weymouth / Braintree branches: DOESN'T MAKE SENSE

- I don't know of any formal study of a Red Line to Hingham or Weymouth. Searches found none. The original South Shore line ended up at Braintree in 1980 ([Railfan](https://railfan.com/boston-mbta-red-line/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_(MBTA))).
- The Greenbush commuter line was fought locally since 1984, with Hingham Square at the heart of it. V: [Patriot Ledger copy](https://ben.penceland.com/CostDerailsGreenbush.html), [BLET on Romney's go-ahead](https://ble-t.org/news/greenbush-mbta-project-gets-romney-ok). Its 2007 opening I could not confirm from these sources.
- JUDGMENT: the commuter rail agent should own this. South Shore density does not support a subway.

### Waltham: FANTASY

- The 1926 report floated Watertown and Waltham, and the 1947 revision had a branch along the Fitchburg Division to Waltham. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_(MBTA))
- Waltham: 65,203 people, a Focus40 "urban gateway." V for population via [citypopulation](https://www.citypopulation.de/en/usa/massachusetts/). Focus40 listing from [search of MBTA documents](https://www.ctps.org/data/calendar/pdfs/2018/MPO_0719_Focus40_Presentation.pdf).
- JUDGMENT: a real city with a real right-of-way, but the Fitchburg Line is the cheaper answer. **Stations:** Porter, Belmont, Waverley, Waltham Center.

### Dudley/Nubian by Red: DOESN'T MAKE SENSE

- I found no plan that sent the Red Line to Dudley. I don't know if one exists.
- JUDGMENT: the Red Line does not pass near Nubian. Orange (old route) or Green (Washington Street) fits.

## 4. Green Line

### Built: Union Square and Medford

Union Square opened March 21, 2022; Medford/Tufts December 12, 2022; cost about $2.28B; forecast 45,000 daily trips in 2030. Cancellation was on the table in December 2015. V: [Wikipedia GLX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_Extension), [Wikipedia Green Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_(MBTA)) (same family of pages, so weak pairing).

### Route 16 / Mystic Valley Parkway: MAKES SENSE

- About 0.9 miles past Medford/Tufts. Dropped in 2009 and deferred, with funds moved to the main project after cost overruns. 2017 Notice of Project Change. V: [Wikipedia GLX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_Extension), [Inside Medford 2011](https://www.insidemedford.com/2011/06/06/green-line-route-16-station-funding-preserved/)

## 6. A new Yellow Line

### What yellow has been used for

- **Yellow was the planned color for what became the Orange Line.** CambridgeSeven tested yellow in 1965 and dropped it. V: [GBH](https://www.wgbh.org/news/local/2022-12-07/how-did-the-mbta-train-lines-get-their-colors), [Wikipedia nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_nomenclature), [Boston Magazine](https://www.bostonmagazine.com/?p=2131084)
- **Conflict on why:** GBH says white lettering did not read on yellow. Wikipedia's bus page says it did not stand out on maps.
- **Yellow later became the bus color**, chosen January 8, 1972, per the MBTA's own 1981 chronicle. ONE SOURCE for the date: [Wikipedia MBTA bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_bus). Other results agree on yellow for bus identity but were not read closely.
- **The official Urban Ring map was drawn in yellow.** The 2009 fact sheet's legend reads "dashed yellow and black line" and "yellow line." ONE SOURCE (primary, read directly): [2009 fact sheet](https://www.cityofboston.gov/Images_Documents/2009%20Urban%20Ring%20Fact%20Sheet_tcm3-29379.pdf). This is the strongest hook for calling your line Yellow.
- **Official "Yellow Line":** I found none. A school newspaper calls the Urban Ring the "Yellow Line." ONE SOURCE and weak: [Brimmer and May Gator](https://bmgator.org/36959/uncategorized/part-5-assessing-proposed-expansions-for-new-lines/)

### Urban Ring: the real study behind it

- Proposed by Boston in 1923, listed in 1926 for a "remote date." ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Ring_Project)
- Phase 2 (bus): 25 miles, 184,000 daily riders, 116,900 already transit users, 41,500 from cars, $2.4B in 2007 dollars, a 1.5-mile tunnel under Longwood, 53% dedicated. ONE SOURCE (primary, read directly): [2009 fact sheet](https://www.cityofboston.gov/Images_Documents/2009%20Urban%20Ring%20Fact%20Sheet_tcm3-29379.pdf)
- Suspended January 2010 on funding. V: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Ring_Project), [state EEA letter](https://eeaonline.eea.state.ma.us/EEA/eMEPA/notices/101006em/14.pdf). (One source says 2009. January 2010 fits the state letter.)
- Phase 3 (rail) ran Assembly to Nubian via East Cambridge. Options: surface light rail, subway light rail, or **a heavy rail Orange Line branch**. Forecast about 290,000 daily riders, which would have made it the second busiest MBTA service. V for ridership: [Rappaport paper 2003](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/2026-08/comingaround.pdf), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Ring_Project)
- Rail cost then guessed at $2B-$3B. ONE SOURCE (2003): Rappaport.
- Caveat, and it is a big one: the same paper warns that most riders would be moved from other lines. Phase 2 added few net new riders. The case is relieving the downtown core. ONE SOURCE: Rappaport.

## Historic fights (the talking points)

- **Arlington, 1976-1980:** a church and school won a 75-yard ban, voters said no to every option, including stopping at Alewife. Half a century later Town Meeting went 145-54 the other way.
- **Lexington:** residents opposed Route 128. ([WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/18/arlington-red-line-extension-proposal-law-mbta))
- **Melrose:** preferred commuter rail, leaving a third track to nowhere (see the source conflict in section 2).
- **Malden and Everett:** Malden residents opposed an elevated extension and wanted a tunnel, leaving Everett as the terminus from 1919. ONE SOURCE: [Wikipedia Everett station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everett_station_(MBTA)). Everett then lost its line to the 1975 reroute. V: [Globe 2025](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/02/11/business/soccer-stadium-everett-public-transit), [Streetsblog](https://mass.streetsblog.org/2025/12/19/should-the-orange-line-to-roslindale-be-the-ts-next-rapid-transit-expansion). **I found no fight by Everett residents themselves.** The story is that Everett got dropped, not that it objected.
- **Southwest Corridor:** homes were cleared for I-95, Gov. Sargent's moratorium came February 11, 1970, and I-95 died by 1972. The land became the Orange Line, tracks and a park, opened May 4, 1987. V: [Wikipedia Southwest Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Corridor_(Massachusetts)), [CommonWealth Beacon](https://commonwealthbeacon.org/energy/sargents-actions-50-years-ago-resonate-today/), [JP Historical Society](https://www.jphs.org/20th-century/how-jamaica-plain-activists-created-the-southwest-corridor.html)
- **Washington Street:** the Elevated closed in 1987. Organizers fought for "equal or better" service. It took until 2002 for the Silver Line, a bus. V: [Wikipedia Elevated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Street_Elevated), [Huntington News](https://huntnewsnu.com/?p=52278). I found no lawsuit.
- **Arborway:** a "temporary" 1985 suspension, tracks paved, suit lost in 2011.
- **A branch:** a "trial" closure in 1969 that never ended.
- **Blue Hill Ave, 2026:** rail tunnel versus a center bus lane with $150M behind it, and 2,150 petition signatures against the lane.
- **GLX:** nearly canceled in 2015, delivered in 2022.

## Hottest takes (all JUDGMENT, each backed above)

1. **Build the Yellow Line before any suburban extension.** The state's own numbers put a ring ahead of nearly anything else, and the state's own 2009 map already drew it in yellow. The counterargument is real: most riders are moved, not new.
2. **Put rail on the new Tobin Bridge.** Chelsea is at 18,381 per square mile and has never had a subway stop.
3. **Roxbury is owed rail, and the Silver Line is an unpaid debt.** A broken 1980s promise outranks suburban wish lists. TransitMatters will hate the tunnel version.
4. **Abolish the Needham Line.** Orange to VFW Parkway, Green to Needham, and the commuter service goes away. Needham's riders lose the one-seat ride to South Station.
5. **Arlington should tear up part of the Minuteman to get the Red Line.** The town that voted down even Alewife may now have to choose between its bike path and its subway.
6. **Stop the Blue Line at Lynn.** People want Salem. Salem is better served by real trains, and overreaching is how Lynn has waited since 1926.
