# Connecticut, Rhode Island and Western Massachusetts: rail research

Research for "What if the MBTA covered all of New England." Researched 2026-11-03.

## How to read this file

Every claim carries one of these tags.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[V]** | Verified: 2 sources agree |
| **[1S]** | ONE SOURCE: treat as unconfirmed under Max's 2-source rule |
| **[P]** | Proposed: a real plan, bill or study idea, not built or funded unless stated |
| **[J]** | My own judgment, not a sourced fact |
| **IDK** | I don't know: I looked and could not confirm it |

Three cautions on source quality.

1. Some sources were read only as search summaries, because the page blocked direct fetching. Those are marked **(snippet)**. Max should treat them as weaker than an opened document.
2. Wikipedia is not truly independent of what it cites. Where it is the second source, the fact counts as [V], but only just.
3. Two [V] facts can trace to one root (for example, both from CTDOT data). That is noted where it matters.

Links use short labels. All URLs are in the source list at the bottom of this file.

## How this region joins the network

[J] Five through-lines would turn the separate services into one system.

1. **Shore Line.** Providence Line continues Wickford Junction, Westerly, New London, New Haven. It joins Metro-North's New Haven Line.
2. **Inland Line.** Boston, Worcester, Springfield, Hartford, New Haven. It is the Inland Route, run as frequent regional rail.
3. **Valley Line.** New Haven, Hartford, Springfield, Holyoke, Northampton, Greenfield. It is today's Hartford Line plus Valley Flyer, run as one line.
4. **Berkshire Line.** Springfield, Pittsfield, Albany, with the Housatonic branch down to Danbury.
5. **Blackstone Line.** Providence, Pawtucket, Woonsocket, Worcester. This is the only new link between Rhode Island and central Massachusetts.

Under one operator, the state-line fights that killed many of these proposals disappear. That matters most for Westerly and the Housatonic line. [J]

## Part 1: Existing services

### 1. New Haven Line main line and New Canaan Branch

- **Owner and operator:** CTDOT owns the Connecticut main line and all three branches. Metro-North operates them. [1S: [wiki-nhl]]
- **Track:** Main line has 4 tracks, 3 between the Housatonic River and Milford. New Canaan Branch is single track, 7.9 miles. [1S: [wiki-nhl]]
- **Electrification:** Main line and New Canaan Branch are electric. [V: [crcog], [wiki-nhl]]
- **Ridership:** Main stem carried 30.3M in 2024, about 25% below 2019. [1S: [ctm-ridership]] Wikipedia gives 32.07M for 2024, which appears to be a wider count that includes branches or the New York section. IDK which definition applies. [1S: [wiki-nhl]]
- **New Canaan:** Over 600,000 riders in 2023. [1S: [ctm-tod]]
- **Plans:** Time for CT aims to cut 25 minutes off New Haven to New York by 2035. [1S: [crcog]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It is the busiest rail line in the region, and it already runs like what the map wants. Keep every stop. [J]

### 2. Waterbury Branch

- **Status:** Single track, 27 miles, diesel. [V for diesel: [crcog], [wiki-nhl]; 1S for length: [wiki-nhl]]
- **Upgrades done:** Signals and passing sidings were finished in 2021, allowing up to 22 trains a weekday. [V: [mta-wby], [mt-wby]]
- **Ridership:** 269,352 in 2024, about 11% above 2019. [1S: [ctm-ridership]]
- **Shutdown:** Buses replace trains July 20, 2026 to May 31, 2027 for station and bridge work at Derby-Shelton, Ansonia, Beacon Falls and Seymour. [V, both trace to CTDOT: [ctdot-wby], [mt-wby2]]
- **Note for the map:** The line is out of service as of this writing, but it returns before any fantasy date. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** The Naugatuck Valley mill towns are a real market, and ridership proves it. Electrify it in the fantasy. [J]

### 3. Danbury Branch

- **Status:** Single track, 23.6 miles, diesel. [V for diesel: [crcog], [wiki-nhl]; 1S for length: [wiki-nhl]]
- **Ridership:** About 300,000 in 2023, roughly half of New Canaan. [1S: [ctm-tod]]
- **Electrification study:** CTDOT has studied the Hartford, Waterbury and Danbury lines since 2023. The report was due by end of 2025 but I found no published result. CRCOG said in June 2026 it was "nearing completion." [V for existence: [ctdot-elec], [crcog]; results IDK]
- **Long-term goal:** A full electric CTrail network, with projects slated for 2036 to 2050. [1S: [crcog]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** Danbury (86,859 people) is a real city at the end of a usable line. Electrify it and extend it (see corridor 16). [J]

### 4. Hartford Line: New Haven, Hartford, Springfield

- **Owner:** Amtrak. [V: [crcog], [nbc-wl]]
- **Ridership:** 733,456 in 2025, down 10% from 817,199 in 2024. [V, both trace to CTDOT data: [crcog], [wiki-hl]]
- **Service:** 22 weekday and 13 weekend round trips, CTrail and Amtrak combined. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Diesel:** Passengers beyond New Haven change trains or wait for an engine swap. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Double track:** Once double along its full length, reduced in the 1990s, now being restored. Phase 3B is $235M, adding 6.2 and 6.7 mile segments, with construction Spring 2027 to Fall 2030. It excludes Hartford Station and the Connecticut River bridge. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Stations under way:** Windsor Locks new station was due to open about October 18, 2026, part of a $121.2M project. [V: [nbc-wl], [crcog]] I did not confirm that it opened. Enfield opens about 2029. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Stations at 30% design or earlier, unfunded:** Windsor relocation, West Hartford, Newington, North Haven. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Bridge blocker:** The 117-year-old Windsor Locks river bridge needs repair or replacement, likely hundreds of millions of dollars. An $8M study was pending in June 2026. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Grade crossings:** 34 along the corridor. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Springfield plan:** 16 round trips between Springfield and New Haven by 2033. [V: [reminder], [pulse-wmass] (snippet)]

**Stops:** New Haven Union, New Haven State Street, North Haven, Wallingford, Meriden, Berlin, Newington, West Hartford, Hartford, Windsor, Windsor Locks, Enfield, Springfield. Existing plus state-planned. [crcog]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It is the backbone of inland New England, and the state has a funded plan. Treat it as the region's main line. [J]

### 5. Shore Line East

- **Owner:** Runs on Amtrak's Northeast Corridor. [V: [tpr-westerly], [crcog]]
- **Ridership:** 193,400 in FY2024, 246,600 in FY2025. Still far below pre-pandemic levels in 2024. [V: [mt-sle], [ctm-ridership]]
- **Subsidy warning:** $183 per rider, against $6.48 on the New Haven Line and $78 on the Hartford Line. [1S: [ctm-funding]]
- **Stamford:** $5M federal funding to add direct trains to Stamford. [1S: [ctm-funding]]
- **Electrified:** Yes. [1S: [crcog]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE, but only as a through-line.** On its own it is the region's costliest service per rider. Extended to Rhode Island it stops being a dead-end branch. [J]

### 6. MBTA Providence Line to Wickford Junction

- **Owner:** Amtrak owns the Northeast Corridor through Rhode Island. RIDOT pays Amtrak access fees for the Providence to Wickford Junction territory and reimburses the MBTA with capital funds. [1S: [ri-appq]]
- **Service:** About 20 weekday round trips to Providence, roughly half going on to Wickford Junction, as of February 2022. [1S: [ri-res]] Current 2026 count IDK.
- **Pawtucket/Central Falls:** Station opened January 2023, with 40 weekday MBTA stops planned. [V: [ridot-pcf], [ridetherails] (snippet)]
- **Wickford Junction:** About 250 daily round trips before the pandemic. [1S: [bpr-ri]]
- **T.F. Green:** A new Amtrak stop at the existing airport station was listed at $180M in Rhode Island's rail project table. [1S: [ri-appq]]
- **Electrify:** Transit Forward RI suggests electrifying Providence to Boston, about 15 minutes faster. [P, 1S: [pvdeye]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It stays exactly as it is. It is the starting point for corridors 7, 8 and 10. [J]

## Part 2: Proposals and corridors

### 7. New London to Westerly (Shore Line East extension)

- **Status:** Amtrak-only corridor. Five Amtrak stops a day at Westerly, about 50,000 passengers a year. [V: [wun-westerly], [mt-sle]]
- **Study:** CTDOT's Eastern Connecticut Corridor Rail and Transit Feasibility Study, 2023. $243M to Westerly, including new stations at Groton, Stonington and Mystic work, plus raising the Westerly platform. [V: [wun-westerly], [tpr-westerly]]
- **Service found feasible:** One train an hour each way. [V: [tpr-westerly], [wun-westerly] (snippet)]
- **Ridership:** One option adds 159,300 riders a year. [1S: [tpr-westerly]] I could not open the study itself, because CTDOT's PDF links now return a 404 error.
- **Recommendation:** The study's recommendation for future study is this extension. [1S: [ctdot-boards] (snippet)]
- **Blocker:** Westerly's track curve makes a full high platform hard. A one-car "mini high" platform is proposed with a $6.5M federal grant, against $20M to $30M for a full one. [1S: [wun-westerly]]
- **Politics:** Governor McKee supports it; Governor Lamont has favored Norwich instead. [1S: [wun-westerly]]
- **Latest:** Open houses on the Connecticut rail plan drew strong support in fall 2026. Updated state rail plan expected winter 2027. [1S: [mt-sle]]

**Stops:** New London, Groton, Mystic, Stonington Borough, Westerly. Advocates also propose a second New London stop near Crescent Street and Pequot Avenue. [P: [mt-sle]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** Tracks are already electrified and in use, and Electric Boat in Groton is a major job site. It is the cheapest big win in this file. [J]

### 8. Westerly to Wickford Junction (the gap)

- **Status:** Amtrak only. Advocates call it the only gap in Northeast commuter rail. [1S: [wun-westerly]]
- **History:** A July 2001 RIDOT report explored MBTA service to Westerly. [1S: [bpr-ri]]
- **Cost:** IDK. I found no current estimate. One older $83M figure for 13 miles of new track and a station south of Wickford Junction appears in a student article, labeled Narragansett, and I can't tell if it is this route. [1S: [bpr-ri]]
- **Timeline:** Officials say the full project would take 5 to 10 years. [1S: [wun-westerly]]
- **Related study:** Amtrak's New Haven to Providence Capacity Planning Study began in 2026, runs about 24 months, and includes inland and shoreline options with no preferred route. [V: [ric-amtrak], [wes-amtrak]]
- **Blocker:** Fast Amtrak trains share the tracks, and the 2017 federal plan dropped a bypass between Old Saybrook and Kenyon after local opposition. [V: [ric-amtrak] (snippet), [crcog]]

**Stops:** Wickford Junction, Kingston (West Kingston historic station), Westerly. [J, station exists per [ri-appq]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** It is 100% existing electrified track and connects two networks that stop within sight of each other on the map. [J]

### 9. New London to Norwich

- **Status:** Freight, on the western Palmer Line tracks. [1S: search summary, page not pinned]
- **Owner:** Genesee & Wyoming through New England Central. [1S: [wiki-ccr]]
- **Cost:** $635.7M, could swing more than 30%. [V: [wun-westerly], [ctptc-sle] (snippet)]
- **Finding:** "Possible given further analysis" but with significant challenges; bus expansion judged more effective for now. [V: [wiki-ccr], [ctptc-sle] (snippet)]
- **Ridership:** IDK. The numbers are in a CTDOT appendix I could not open.

**Stops:** New London, Mohegan (casino), Norwich. [P: [wiki-ccr]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** Norwich has 40,000 people and Mohegan Sun is a real destination, but it costs nearly 3 times the Westerly extension. Build it only as the south end of the Central Corridor (corridor 23). [J]

### 10. Providence to Woonsocket, on to Worcester

- **Status:** Freight, Providence & Worcester main line, owned by Genesee & Wyoming. [1S: [wiki-bsr]]
- **History:** Passenger service ended 1960. [1S: [wiki-blackstone]]
- **Private attempt:** Boston Surface Railroad planned 2 daily round trips, Providence to Worcester, about 65 to 70 minutes. It went bankrupt in 2019, left Woonsocket Depot in January 2021, and was dissolved in September 2023. Service never ran. [1S: [wiki-bsr]]
- **State plans:** Rhode Island's 2020-era plan table lists track work at Valley Falls that "will enhance freight and potential passenger rail service." RIDOT keeps Woonsocket's historic station maintained. [1S: [ri-appq]]
- **Transit plan:** Scenarios used regional rapid bus for Providence to Woonsocket rather than rail. [1S: [sambuz-tmp] (snippet)]
- **Blocker:** Woonsocket's Main Street rail viaduct is poorly rated with limited weight capacity. [1S: [ri-appq]] Rhode Island also wants more clearance for double-stack freight from Pawtucket to Quonset. [1S: [ri-appq]]
- **Population:** Woonsocket 43,227, Pawtucket 75,641, Central Falls 22,586 on just 1.2 sq mi, about 19,000 per square mile. [1S: [cp-ri], computed density]

**Stops:** Providence, Pawtucket/Central Falls, Valley Falls/Cumberland, Lincoln (Lonsdale), Manville, Woonsocket, then Blackstone, Uxbridge, Millbury, Worcester. [J, drawn from the historic line; one advocate's plan puts Pawtucket about 19 minutes from Woonsocket: [ped-obs]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** A dense mill-city corridor with tracks already in place, and it links New England's 2nd and 3rd largest cities. The failed private operator shows a weak company, not a weak corridor. [J]

### 11. Fall River to Newport (and Aquidneck Island)

- **Fall River today:** South Coast Rail opened March 24, 2025. Fall River/New Bedford Line weekday ridership rose from about 6,500 to about 10,000. [V: [tt10-scr], [wiki-scr] (snippet)]
- **Broken link:** The Sakonnet River rail bridge closed in 1988 and was removed in 2007, ending the island's rail link. Some sources give other closing dates. [V: [bpr-ri], [wiki-sakonnet]]
- **Owner:** RIDOT owns the Newport Secondary. [1S: [wiki-newport-sec] (snippet)]
- **Today:** Newport & Narragansett Bay Railroad runs heritage trains on the island. [V: [wror], [wiki-ri-rr]]
- **Rail-with-trail:** By 2024 the second track was removed for the Aquidneck Island bikeway. [1S: [wiki-newport-sec] (snippet)]
- **Study:** A 1994 RIDOT study found Newport to Fall River had the lowest capital cost of 9 corridors but the highest cost per rider. [1S: [bpr-ri]]
- **Scale:** About 16.3 miles of new track plus upgrades. Tracks also run through Naval Station Newport. About 950 Newport workers commuted to cities on the line (2011 to 2015 data). [1S: [bpr-ri]]
- **Interest:** A 2024 compatible-use study was told to consider a Fall River station connection. [1S: [planetizen-aq]]
- **Population:** Newport 25,164. [1S: [cp-ri]]

**Stops:** Fall River, Tiverton, Portsmouth, Middletown, Newport. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** Newport's summer tourism could carry it, but it needs a new bridge and a weekend-heavy market. A CapeFLYER-style seasonal line fits better than daily commuter service. [J]

### 12. Springfield to Greenfield (Knowledge Corridor)

- **Owner:** MassDOT, bought from Pan Am. [V: [wiki-crl], [wamc-2025] (snippet)]
- **Rebuild:** $73M federal plus $10M state. [1S: [wiki-crl]]
- **Service:** 3 round trips Springfield to Greenfield, shared by Valley Flyer and Vermonter. [1S: [reminder]]
- **Ridership:** Valley Flyer and Vermonter together logged 56,369 station activity in FY2024, up 16%. [1S: [tiv-stations] (snippet)] Massachusetts ridership overall rose 99% from 2018 to 2024. [1S: [compass]]
- **Freight:** Local freight from East Deerfield. [1S: [tiv-freight] (snippet)]
- **Proposals:** Bills H.3532, H.3777 and H.3418 ask to study extending New Haven-Hartford-Springfield service through Holyoke, Northampton and Greenfield. I found no sign they passed. [P, V for existence: [ma-h3532], [ma-h3777]]
- **Added frequency:** No specific Greenfield figure found. IDK.
- **Track count:** Mostly single track is my understanding, not confirmed. IDK.

**Stops:** Springfield, Chicopee, Holyoke, Northampton, South Deerfield, Greenfield. Holyoke, Northampton, Greenfield exist; the rest are mine. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** The line is state-owned and already rebuilt, and Northampton and Amherst-area colleges are strong markets. 3 trains a day is the only thing missing. [J]

### 13. Springfield to Pittsfield

- **Owner:** CSX. [V: [wiki-ewr], [compass]]
- **Service:** Amtrak's Lake Shore Limited Boston section stops at Springfield and Pittsfield. A January 2026 guide calls it daily, and it returned after a 2025 closure. [V: [tiv-lsl] (snippet), [railpace-lsl] (snippet)] One source says "no round trips" between Springfield and Pittsfield, probably meaning state-supported trains only. [conflict: [reminder]]
- **Berkshire Flyer:** Seasonal New York to Pittsfield since 2022. MassDOT and CSX reached an October 2026 agreement making it permanent seasonal service, with $31M in the 2027 to 2031 plan, a siding near Pittsfield, and a platform study. 2026 added a second round trip. [V: [mt-flyer], [wiki-flyer]]
- **Siding:** About 14,000 feet in Richmond, design complete. [1S: [compass]]
- **Flyer ridership:** I found 826 for FY24 on Wikipedia, which looks like a partial count. Treat as unreliable. [1S: [wiki-flyer]]
- **East-West study 2021:** Pittsfield-Boston alternatives cost $2.4B, $3.9B and $4.6B, with Pittsfield to Boston in 2:49 to 3:09. [V for $2.4B: [wiki-ewr], [reminder-ewr] (snippet); the other two 1S: [wiki-ewr]] An earlier presentation put the $2.4B option at 922 to 1,188 weekday riders. [1S: [reminder-ewr] (snippet)]
- **Compass Rail plan:** 2 Boston to New Haven round trips in 2030, more Hartford Line service in 2033, Boston to Albany in 2045. [V: [compass], [reminder]]
- **Stops:** No added stops between Springfield and Pittsfield currently planned; a study is planned. [1S: [reminder]] Chester was in the 2021 study, later cut. [1S: [wiki-ewr]]

**Stops:** Springfield, Westfield, Chester, Pittsfield. Chester from the 2021 study, Westfield mine (40,814 people). [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** The track is busy and active, the state has a phased plan, and Pittsfield is the Berkshires' only real city. Hilly single track makes it slow, so accept 2 to 3 hour trips to Boston. [J]

### 14. Pittsfield to Albany

- **Status:** Same CSX line. [V: [wiki-ewr], [compass]]
- **Plan:** Phase III, 2045: 5 Boston to Springfield and 2 Springfield to Albany round trips. Needs 2 to 3 more trainsets, South Station Expansion and track work. [V: [compass], [reminder]]
- **Corridor program:** The Boston & Albany corridor is in the federal Corridor ID program, with a $3.5M Step 2 service plan grant. [V: [compass], [railwayage-sdp] (snippet)]
- **Gap:** Earlier studies stopped at Pittsfield, so the Albany segment is the least studied. [1S: [wiki-ewr]]

**Stops:** Pittsfield, Chatham (NY), Albany-Rensselaer. Chatham is my guess. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** Albany is outside New England, but it is the region's rail door to the west and the Empire Corridor. End the line here. [J]

### 15. Greenfield to North Adams (Northern Tier, Hoosac Line)

Likely overlaps the central Massachusetts research. Only the western end is covered here.

- **Owner:** Pan Am Southern, jointly owned by Norfolk Southern and CSX; run by Berkshire & Eastern, a Genesee & Wyoming company, since CSX bought Pan Am in 2022. [V: [wiki-pas], [tiv-freight] (snippet)]
- **MassDOT study (Nov 2024):** 6 options. Capital $878M to $2.9B. Riders 66,000 to 304,000 a year. Top option is electrified, 2:50 North Adams to Boston, about equal to driving. Five trains a day. Service 12 years after planning money. Most riders are east of Fitchburg. [V: [gazette-nt], [scd-nt]]
- **Tunnel:** Hoosac Tunnel, about 4.75 miles, in freight use. [1S: photo caption in a search summary, page not pinned]

**Stops:** Greenfield, Shelburne Falls, Charlemont, North Adams. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** The best option costs about $2.9B for roughly 540 to 830 riders a day. The map can have it, but the numbers say west of Fitchburg is scenery, not commuting. [J]

### 16. Danbury to Pittsfield (Housatonic / Berkshire Line)

- **Status:** Active freight, Housatonic Railroad, headquartered in Canaan, CT. Freight customers in both states. [V: [hrrc-sys], [hrrc-pass]]
- **Ownership in Massachusetts:** MassDOT's board approved buying the 37 miles to the Connecticut line in 2014 for $12.1M, plus $35M in first track work. Housatonic keeps a permanent freight easement. [V: [pr-berk], [berkshire-edge-2018]] IDK whether the sale ever closed; I found no confirmation.
- **Commitment claim:** The railroad says Massachusetts committed $113.8M to upgrade its part. [1S: [hrrc-pass]]
- **Cost:** About $200M for Pittsfield to Danbury (reported 2018, surely higher now). [1S: [berkshire-edge-2018]] Earlier railroad estimates ran lower. [1S: [wamc-hrrc] (snippet)]
- **Connecticut position:** MassDOT rated it Tier 3 and "low" in 2018, noting no likelihood of Connecticut improvements. Governor Malloy said it mainly helps Massachusetts. [1S: [berkshire-edge-2018]]
- **Danbury end:** Metro-North stops at Danbury, about 15 miles short of New Milford. [1S: [ctm-funding]] HB 7059 (2025) would have studied hybrid trains and New Milford, about $2M, due February 2026. Last action I found was tabled May 7, 2025, with no published report. [V for bill: [cga-7059], [fd-7059]]
- **Older New Milford costs:** $400M Danbury electrification and $450M New Milford extension, from a 30-year plan, year unknown. [1S: [ct-senate-gop] (snippet)]

**Warning:** The Berkshire Edge site shows 2026 dates in its page header that are crawl dates. The article I read is from February 2018. Search summaries that date it 2026 are wrong.

**Stops, from the railroad's own list:** Danbury, Brookfield/Danbury North, New Milford, Kent, Cornwall Bridge, a regional stop for Sheffield and North Canaan, Great Barrington, Stockbridge, Lee, Lenox, Pittsfield. [P: [hrrc-pass]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** The track exists, freight is light, and it is the one direct Berkshires to New York line. Under one regional operator the Connecticut objection disappears, so this is the best "fantasy fixes the politics" line on the map. [J]

### 17. Windsor Locks to Bradley Airport

- **Today:** Shuttle bus every 20 minutes weekdays, 12 minutes to Terminal A. [V: [nbc-wl], [crcog]]
- **Study:** Bradley Airport Light Rail Feasibility Study, March 2016. Four routes, $56M to $97M, about $9.9M to $14.3M per mile. The Route 20 light rail scored highest. The study named no preferred option. [1S, opened in full: [bradley-2016]]
- **History:** A 1974 people mover at the airport never opened and was demolished. [1S: [wiki-bpm]]

**Stops:** Windsor Locks, Bradley Terminal. [bradley-2016]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** Cheap by this file's standards, but a 12-minute bus already does the job. A short light rail shuttle is a nice map flourish and airport rail always draws attention. [J]

### 18. Hartford to Bradley via Griffin Line

- **HB 5061 (2025):** Would have required rail from Hartford Union Station to Bradley with stops in Hartford, Bloomfield and Windsor Locks. Listed dead June 4, 2025. [V for bill: [cga-5061], [trains-5061]; outcome 1S: [legiplex-5061] (snippet)]
- **History:** Griffin Line Major Investment Study 1995; Griffin Busway Feasibility Study 2004 said preserve the corridor but defer a busway indefinitely. [1S: [bradley-2016]]
- **Owner:** IDK.

**Stops:** Hartford, Blue Hills, Bloomfield (Griffin Center), Bradley Terminal. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** It serves Hartford's North End and suburban office parks, not just the airport. Best built as part of a Hartford light rail system (Part 3). [J]

### 19. Hartford to Worcester, direct

- **Corridor:** No direct rail line exists that I could find. [J]
- **Advocacy:** The goal is Hartford Line trains reaching Worcester, via Springfield. A CRCOG and Pioneer Valley study found nearly 10-to-1 economic return on New Haven to Worcester investment. [V: [westernmass-econ] (snippet), [wamc-econ] (snippet)]

**Verdict: DOESN'T MAKE SENSE as a new line.** Springfield is already on the way, and new right-of-way through empty hills would save little. Corridor 20 does the same job. [J]

### 20. Springfield to Worcester (Inland Route)

The eastern half is someone else's region; this section is here because Hartford depends on it.

- **Owner:** CSX. [V: [wiki-ewr], [compass]]
- **Track:** About 45 of the 55 miles are single track. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Funding:** $108M federal award. [V: [compass], [reminder]] CRCOG reports another $27M for $135M total, and a 2029 target, while MassDOT's 2026 deck says 2030. [conflict: [crcog], [compass]]
- **Plan:** Design 2025, construction 2027, 2 Boston to New Haven round trips in 2030. Boston to Springfield falls from 2:30+ to 2:00 to 2:10. [V: [compass], [wiki-ewr]]
- **New threat:** MassDOT says Amtrak's current fleet is no longer reliable to run it, so service needs 2 Airo trainsets. [1S: [compass]]
- **Palmer station:** Site chosen January 2025 at 1199 South Main Street, 2033 target. Could offer a UMass bus link. [V: [compass], [reminder]]

**Stops:** Springfield, Palmer, Worcester. [compass]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE.** This is the single link that makes New England feel like one network. It connects Boston to Hartford without a change at New Haven. [J]

### 21. Hartford to Waterbury via New Britain and Bristol

- **Status:** Historic Highland Line. Parts remain in freight use; a 2005 account says Southington to Plainville only. Current status IDK. [1S: [nhrhta-highland] (snippet)]
- **Study:** Central Connecticut Rail Study, 2016 to 2017. Passenger rail on the 24-mile Berlin to Waterbury corridor judged cost prohibitive: $410M to $530M. Press reports said no more than 800 riders a day. Freight investment judged viable. [1S: [rtands-ccrs] (snippet)]
- **Population:** Waterbury 114,452, New Britain 74,151. [1S: [cp-ct]] Waterbury is Connecticut's 5th largest city. [J]

**Stops:** Berlin, New Britain, Plainville, Bristol, Terryville, Waterbury. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** The state did the math and it failed. But no rail line joins Waterbury to Hartford today without going through New Haven, so a network map wants it. [J]

### 22. Hartford to Willimantic

- **Status:** Abandoned as a railroad. Hop River State Park Trail runs 20.2 miles through Manchester, Vernon, Bolton, Coventry, Andover and Columbia. Last passenger train 1970. [V: [ct-hop], [tl-hop] (snippet)]
- **Connection:** Bridges into the Air Line trail at Willimantic. [1S: [ct-hop] (snippet)]
- **Ownership:** IDK; the sources call it a state park trail but don't state who owns the land.
- **Proposals:** None found for passenger rail. Amtrak's capacity study covers the area bounded by Hartford and Providence. [1S: [crcog]]

**Stops:** Hartford, East Hartford, Manchester, Bolton Notch, Willimantic, with UConn by bus. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** It is the obvious route for a Hartford to Providence inland line, and Manchester is a real suburb. It means taking a popular trail, though rail-with-trail could soften that. [J]

### 23. New London to Brattleboro (Central Corridor)

- **Status:** Active freight, New England Central, owned by Genesee & Wyoming since 2012. [1S: [wiki-ccr]]
- **Proposal:** 10 stations: New London, Mohegan, Norwich, Willimantic, Mansfield/Storrs, Stafford Springs, Palmer, Amherst, Millers Falls, Brattleboro. [V: [wiki-ccr], [mt-central] (snippet)]
- **Cost:** About $150M including track, stations and rolling stock, circa 2010 to 2012. Far out of date. [1S: [wiki-ccr]]
- **Study:** MassDOT feasibility study, 2017; no further action. Findings IDK (mass.gov blocks automated fetching). [1S: [wiki-ccr]]
- **Passenger history:** Ended 1947; Vermonter stopped at Amherst until 2014. [V: [wiki-nlnr], [wiki-amherst]]
- **Railroad stance:** "Open to a conversation." [1S: [wiki-ccr] (snippet)]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** Two flagship state universities and a casino sit on one existing line. Rural miles between them keep it from penciling out in the real world. [J]

### 24. Norwich to Worcester via Putnam

- **Status:** My understanding is freight on the Norwich Branch under Genesee & Wyoming, but I did not confirm it. IDK.
- **History:** Budd cars once ran Worcester to New London twice a day. [1S: [mt-putnam]]
- **Local interest:** 93% of 469 Putnam survey responses wanted passenger rail. A self-selected survey. [1S: [mt-putnam]]
- **Study:** None found. Putnam wants inclusion in Amtrak's capacity study. [1S: [mt-putnam]]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** Thin population, but it connects southeastern Connecticut to Worcester on existing track. [J]

### 25. Waterbury to Torrington

- **Status:** CTDOT has owned Devon to Torrington since 1982. Leased to Naugatuck Railroad, the Railroad Museum of New England's tourist operation. [V: [wiki-naug], [rmne]]
- **Passenger history:** Ended 1958. [1S: [wiki-naug-old] (snippet)]
- **Proposals:** Politicians mention Thomaston and Torrington as future stops. No funded plan found. [1S: [ct-sengop-wby] (snippet)]

**Stops:** Waterbury, Thomaston, Torrington. [J]

**Verdict: MAKES SENSE ONLY IN FANTASY.** The state already owns it, so it is the easiest branch extension in Connecticut. Demand is unproven. [J]

### 26. New Haven to Northampton (Canal Line)

- **Status:** Rail trail. Farmington Canal Heritage Trail, 82 to 84 miles planned, with a gap between Westfield and Southampton as of 2025. [V: [nh-canal], [wiki-canal]]
- **History:** Former New Haven & Northampton Railroad. [1S: [tl-canal] (snippet)]

**Verdict: DOESN'T MAKE SENSE.** It parallels the Hartford Line, and it is a popular trail. New Haven's end may suit a short light rail, nothing more. [J]

### Providence: YES, light rail

- **Metro Connector study:** About $1.7M, began 2024. Four options. Light rail runs Central Falls, Pawtucket, Providence to Park Avenue in Cranston, in two versions that differ on Dexter or Broad Street, the downtown route, and Broad Street or Elmwood Avenue. Rapid bus options reach CCRI Warwick or T.F. Green. [V for options and cost: [globe-ri], [ripta-study] (snippet)]
- **Numbers:** Light rail over $1B, 17,000 to 19,000 daily riders by 2035. Rapid bus $322M to $365M, 9,000 to 11,000. Light rail stops at Park Avenue because density falls south of it. Pick expected 2027. [1S for ridership: [globe-ri]]
- **Blocker:** Depends on federal Capital Investment Grants, stalled under the current administration. [1S: [globe-ri]]
- **Transit Forward RI 2040:** Adopted December 2020. Two rapid lines that would be light rail if built, six more Rapid Bus lines including Elmwood, Chalkstone and Cranston Street. [V: [pvdeye], [ripta-award] (snippet)] Capital $1.9B to $3.1B over 20 years. [1S: search summary, article not pinned]
- **Dead streetcar:** A 2-mile College Hill to Upper South Providence line grew from $76M to about $114M to $126M. The city lost one federal TIGER bid and reapplied; the second outcome is IDK. Dropped for an enhanced bus at about $20M. [V: [bb-streetcar], [nextcity-pvd]]
- **Old network:** United Electric Railways ran Providence and Woonsocket lines 1921 to 1951. Streetcars ended 1948. The 1914 East Side Trolley Tunnel still carries RIPTA buses. [V: [wiki-uer], [osm-trolley]]

**Suggested system [J]:**

- **Line 1, north-south (from the study):** Central Falls, Pawtucket, North Main, Providence Station, Kennedy Plaza, Hospital District, Broad Street or Elmwood, Park Avenue Cranston.
- **Line 2, east-west:** Thayer Street through the East Side Tunnel, Kennedy Plaza, Federal Hill, Olneyville, Manton. It reuses a real trolley tunnel, which makes a lovely map detail.
- **Anchors:** Brown, Rhode Island Hospital, Pawtucket/Central Falls Station.

### Hartford: FANTASY, light rail only

- **No light rail or subway proposal found.** I searched specifically and got nothing. A CTfastrak to rail conversion is my idea, not a real proposal. IDK of anyone proposing it.
- **CTfastrak:** 9.4-mile busway, New Britain to Hartford, 10 stations, built for $567M, partly on an abandoned rail line and beside an Amtrak line. [V: [nextcity-brt], [nbc-busway]] 28.5M trips in its first 10 years. [1S: [ctdot-fastrak]] About 14,000 a day in 2015-2016. Current daily IDK. [1S: [metro-fastrak] (snippet)]
- **East:** Expansion to East Hartford and Manchester runs as buses on I-84 HOV lanes, not a new guideway. [V: [ctdot-fastrak-east], [hb-fastrak]]
- **Greater Hartford Mobility Study (2020 to 2023):** Lower I-84, move the I-84/I-91 interchange, new station between Asylum Hill and downtown, rail viaduct moved. About $4B to $6B for City Link West alone. [V: [ghms-crcog], [nbc-ghms]]
- **Station:** Realigning and double-tracking 2.1 miles of rail requires replacing Union Station as a station. $2.56M planning grant, 2024. [1S: [crcog]]
- **Old network:** More than 125 miles of track by the Hartford division's peak, radiating in all directions. Last car July 1941. [V: [shoreline-hfd], [windsor-hist]]

**Suggested system [J]:**

- **Line 1, New Britain to Manchester:** Convert CTfastrak to light rail, New Britain, Newington, Elmwood, Parkville, new Hartford Station, downtown, East Hartford (Pratt & Whitney), Manchester (Buckland).
- **Line 2, Griffin to Wethersfield:** Griffin Center, Blue Hills, North End, new Hartford Station, downtown, Trinity College, Hartford Hospital, Wethersfield Avenue. The last leg follows a real trolley corridor.
- **Why no subway:** Density of 6,965 per square mile is respectable, but the city has only 121,000 people. Surface rail on a cleared right-of-way is enough.

### New Haven: FANTASY, small light rail

- **Streetcar:** A 2008 streetcar assessment became Move New Haven in 2016. [V: [nhi-streetcar], [scrcog-sc]] A 1.5-mile downtown loop covered Union Station, the medical district and Science Hill. [1S: [ydn-streetcar]] The Board of Alders rejected a study in 2011 and 2012. [1S: [nhi-streetcar]]
- **Move New Haven BRT:** $307M, 4 busiest routes (212, 238, 246, 265), starting 2028, finishing 2030. $25M federal grant. Phase 2 started June 2026. [V: [gmt-brt], [vhb-brt]]
- **Old network:** Last trolley about September 25, 1948. Shore Line Trolley Museum in East Haven runs on the old right-of-way. [V: [shoreline-nh], [wiki-slt]]

**Suggested system [J]:**

- **Line 1:** Union Station, downtown, Yale, Dixwell, Hamden Plaza. Uses the canal-line corridor north of downtown.
- **Line 2:** Westville, Whalley Avenue, downtown, Grand Avenue, Fair Haven, East Haven, joining the trolley museum right-of-way.
- **Anchors:** Yale, Yale New Haven Hospital, Union Station, Southern Connecticut State.
- **Why no subway:** 134,000 people. BRT on the same four routes is the honest answer; light rail is the fantasy upgrade.

### Springfield: NO

- **No light rail or streetcar proposal found.** IDK of any.
- **Old network:** Springfield Street Railway ran interurbans until 1940, buses until 1981. Holyoke Street Railway ended streetcars in 1937. [V: [wiki-ssr], [wiki-hsr]]
- **Why not:** Density of 4,893 per square mile. Holyoke (1,807) and Chicopee (2,426) are thinner. [computed from [cp-ma]] The urban area is long and thin along the river, which suits trains, not a metro. [J]

**Suggested instead [J]:** Run the Valley Line (corridor 12) and Inland Line every 30 minutes, with infill stops at Chicopee and Longmeadow. That is a Springfield metro in everything but name. A single State Street streetcar from Union Station to Springfield College and American International College is the most a fantasy could justify.

### Bridgeport and Stamford

- **Bridgeport:** 148,628 people, about 9,250 per square mile, the densest big city in Connecticut. No rail transit proposal found. Trolleys ended 1937. [V for trolley: [shoreline-co], [wiki-slt] (snippet)] Fantasy: one short line from Bridgeport Station up Main Street to Trumbull. Not studied. [J]
- **Stamford:** About 3,600 per square mile, and trolleys ended 1933. A WestCOG study reviewed station shuttles. [1S: [westcog]] Verdict: NO. Keep it a strong Metro-North node with good buses. [J]
- **Statewide:** A 2009 bill sought a light rail feasibility review; outcome IDK. [1S: [ct-sen-lr] (snippet)]
